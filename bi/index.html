IFDC Burundi Updates Agricultural Curricula for Technical Schools

New teaching materials developed by IFDC and the Ministry of Education aim to enhance the quality of agricultural training across Burundi.

The International Fertilizer Development Center (IFDC) in Burundi, in partnership with the Ministry of National Education and Scientific Research, organized a workshop on August 25, 2026, to validate updated curricula for plant production, earth sciences, and socio-extension services. These revised materials are intended for the Agricultural Technical Institutes of Burundi (ITAB). Micaël BEUN, IFDC's Pagris project coordinator, stated that the improved curricula incorporate IFDC's innovative approaches to strengthen the quality of agricultural technical education.

Each course will now feature three components: a teacher's guide, a student manual, and a practical work guide. BEUN highlighted IFDC's role as a knowledge and expertise center in Burundi, particularly in soil and plant health, with the goal of sustainably increasing agricultural production. IFDC has been active in Burundi since 2006, developing specific expertise in soil health, the seed sector, and assisting households and communities in managing their farms and watersheds more effectively.

This initiative is part of the strategy to sustain the PSSD2 and Pagris projects, funded by the Netherlands Embassy in Burundi. IFDC Burundi is working to consolidate and disseminate the achievements of these projects, ensuring national stakeholders adopt the results. This effort relies on close collaboration among development actors and aims to provide lasting support to Burundi's educational system, especially agricultural technical education.

Why it mattersAgricultural technical schools and their students are directly affected by the updated curricula designed to improve the quality of training.

ARMC Launches University Challenge to Boost Financial Literacy

Burundi's Capital Markets Authority aims to educate young people about financial markets through a nationwide competition.

The Capital Markets Authority of Burundi (ARMC) has launched its 2026 University Challenge, a competition designed to engage students from all disciplines across public and private universities. Dr. Arsène Mugenzi, ARMC's Director General, stated the initiative seeks to enhance financial education and awareness among university students, fostering a generation better informed about the financial sector, particularly capital markets. He emphasized that understanding these markets is crucial for their effective functioning and for students to benefit from them.

This competition follows a series of awareness campaigns conducted by ARMC in various universities, both in Bujumbura and nationwide. During these sessions, students received training on topics including the role of capital markets in the economy, key market players, financial instruments, the stock exchange, investment risks and opportunities, and how to participate in the challenge. As of August 26, 2026, over 800 students had registered, with applications closing on August 30.

Beyond its educational goals, the ARMC aims to recognize student engagement and excellence through prizes. Top performers will receive rewards such as computers, smartphones, tablets, financial securities, and cash. Additionally, outstanding candidates will be offered professional internships with approved capital market firms, providing practical immersion and career development opportunities within the financial ecosystem. The inclusion of financial securities as prizes is intended to encourage direct student engagement with investment mechanisms.

Why it mattersUniversity students in Burundi are affected by this competition as it offers them educational opportunities, potential prizes, and internships in the financial sector.

Business · 2026-08-30

Fires Destroy Bujumbura Market Stalls and Galleries

Hundreds of merchants face ruin after three separate fires in less than a week destroyed commercial infrastructure in the capital city.

Fires ravaged the Ndayizamba and Le Parisien galleries, and a section of the Kinindo market in Bujumbura within days, leaving hundreds of merchants in distress and raising concerns about commercial infrastructure safety. The August 21 blaze at ETS Ndayizamba gallery quickly spread to the adjacent Le Parisien gallery, reducing shops, merchandise, and equipment to ashes. Days later, on August 25, a fire broke out in a section of the Kinindo market known for mattress sales, its flammable nature reportedly accelerating the flames.

Panic ensued during both incidents as merchants attempted to salvage goods, while others watched years of work and investment turn to ash. Emergency services battled the blazes for hours, but the rapid spread and intensity made intervention difficult. The exact causes of the fires and the factors contributing to their rapid propagation remain under official investigation.

The destruction has plunged numerous traders into despair, with many witnessing the loss of their livelihoods. The fires highlight potential vulnerabilities in the city's commercial centers, impacting the economic stability of many small business owners and their families who rely on these enterprises for income.

Why it mattersHundreds of merchants are affected by the destruction of their shops and merchandise, leading to significant financial losses and distress.
