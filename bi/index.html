Seven opposition parties boycott Burundi's election-commission meeting

Seven opposition parties, including the National Congress for Freedom, boycotted or walked out of a preparatory meeting held by Burundi's Independent National Electoral Commission (CENI) for the 2027 presidential election, according to the International Crisis Group. The parties denounced what they called CENI's refusal to acknowledge irregularities in the 2025 legislative elections.

Why it mattersEarly opposition distrust of the election body signals a contested run-up to 2027, a factor foreign observers and investors will watch for stability risk.

More than 71,000 Congolese refugees have crossed into Burundi since January

The United Nations says more than 71,000 people fleeing violence in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo have crossed into Burundi since January, according to Al Jazeera. Arrivals have strained camps such as Musenyi in southeastern Burundi, where aid agencies have been distributing food and cooking oil from dwindling supplies.

Why it mattersA sustained refugee influx adds pressure on food, aid budgets and public services in one of the world's poorest countries.

Burundi inflation eases to 11.4% in February, lowest reading since 2022

Burundi's annual inflation rate fell to 11.4% in February 2026 from 12.6% in January, marking the lowest level since January 2022, according to Trading Economics data from the national statistics institute ISTEEBU. Food inflation stood at 27% year-on-year in February, down from 29.9% the prior month. Forecasters expect the headline rate to end the quarter near 14%.

Why it mattersSlower price growth offers some relief to households after inflation topped 45% in April 2025, though food costs remain sharply elevated.

Burundi's widening exchange-rate gap fuels shortages, experts urge reform

Burundi's widening gap between official and parallel exchange rates has distorted the economy for years, with experts calling for reforms to address shortages and rising prices, according to Breaking Burundi. The African Development Bank projects the current-account deficit will narrow to 6.3% of GDP in 2026 from prior levels, helped by gold and coffee exports, but warns the outlook remains vulnerable to fuel and foreign-currency constraints.

Why it mattersThe currency distortion is a key driver of fuel shortages and import costs that ripple through prices for everyday goods.
