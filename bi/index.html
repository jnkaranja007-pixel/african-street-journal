Bodies Found in Gitega Province Spark Vigilantism Concerns

Two new deaths, one from alleged theft and another of an infant, have raised alarms about escalating mob justice and unexplained fatalities in Burundi's Gitega province.

Two bodies were discovered in Gitega province on August 24, fueling concerns over vigilantism and unexplained deaths. Népomucène Ntakarutimana was reportedly beaten to death by residents after being caught attempting to steal rabbits. The local hill chief, Jean Claude Mfukamensabe, confirmed the incident and stated that three individuals, Jacqueline Ndayishimiye, Anitha Nshimirimana, and Arsène Ciza, have been arrested in connection with the beating and are in police custody for investigation.

On the same day, the body of a male infant was found in the Karonga valley, separating the Yoba and Rukoba neighborhoods. Gitega police confirmed the discovery, but the circumstances surrounding the baby's death and the identity of any responsible parties remain unknown. The infant was buried locally pending further investigation into the cause of death and the identification of potential culprits.

The following day, August 25, two more deaths occurred in the Bugendana commune under separate circumstances. The Bugendana police post chief has been arrested as part of the ongoing investigations into these events. These incidents collectively highlight a rise in summary executions and mob justice in the province, alongside concerns about the methods employed by security forces.

Why it mattersThe discovery of bodies and subsequent arrests in Gitega province highlight a rise in vigilantism and unexplained deaths, impacting community safety and the justice system.

Burundi Travelers Face Scrutiny, Extortion on Rwanda Route

Travelers and transport operators between Burundi and Rwanda report increased interrogations, alleged extortion, and fear of intimidation at border crossings.

Burundian and Congolese travelers heading to Rwanda are experiencing heightened scrutiny, repeated questioning, and alleged intimidation at border checkpoints. Agents from the National Intelligence Service, supported by ruling party youth league members, are reportedly focusing on individuals destined for Rwanda, questioning their motives, contacts, and duration of stay. Some travelers feel their destination makes them targets, with one noting that going to Rwanda has become "like a sacrilege."

Currency exchangers in the border zone observe that travelers bound for Rwanda face more intense questioning and searches than those traveling to Tanzania. This perceived differential treatment leads some travelers to conceal their true destination, falsely stating they are going to Tanzania to avoid unwanted attention from authorities. Taxi drivers operating between Burundi's capital and Kigali also report facing suspicion and threats, accused of transporting young people who might join the armed group RED-Tabara, which Burundi suspects Kigali supports.

The increased scrutiny has impacted transport operations, with some drivers allegedly forced to pay significant sums to continue their routes. One driver reported paying up to one million Burundian francs at the Muyinga police station to maintain service to Rwanda. Faced with these pressures and accusations, some transport operators are reportedly changing vehicles or suspending their services to avoid being systematically suspected and to mitigate financial demands.

Why it mattersTravelers and transport operators are affected by increased interrogations and alleged extortion when traveling to Rwanda, impacting their freedom of movement and financial stability.

Burundi Court Grants Provisional Liberty With Movement Restrictions

An accused individual in Burundi has been granted provisional liberty, subject to strict conditions including limitations on travel and contact with others.

A court in Burundi has granted provisional liberty to an accused individual, imposing specific movement restrictions. The court order stipulates that the accused must not obstruct the ongoing investigation or cause scandal through their conduct. This measure aims to balance the individual's liberty with the needs of the judicial process.

The restrictions include prohibitions on accessing specific locations such as airports, ports, and train stations, or being present at these places during designated times. Furthermore, the accused is required to report periodically to an investigating magistrate or a designated official. They must also appear before the court whenever summoned and are forbidden from contacting victims or other specified individuals.

The court's order details the precise conditions of the provisional liberty, noting that not all restrictions may apply simultaneously. A copy of the provisional liberty order is to be provided to the accused, who must sign it after notification. This notification must be completed within twenty-four hours of the order being issued.

Why it mattersThe restrictions on movement and contact directly impact individuals facing legal proceedings, potentially affecting their ability to travel for business or personal reasons.

General Gasita Returns to Eastern DRC Command After Deadly Uvira Protests

Brigade General Olivier Gasita, previously removed from Uvira following violent demonstrations in 2025, has been reassigned to intelligence duties in Lubumbashi.

Brigade General Olivier Gasita has returned to the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, taking up intelligence responsibilities in Lubumbashi. His new role within the 2nd Defense Zone of the FARDC comes less than a year after deadly protests in Uvira forced his recall to Kinshasa. General Gasita arrived in Lubumbashi on Saturday, August 22, alongside Major General Ngoie Kilubi Bob, to assume his duties overseeing military intelligence for a region encompassing multiple provinces.

Gasita's previous assignment in Uvira began in September 2025 as head of operations and military intelligence for the 33rd Military Region. His presence there quickly drew significant opposition from segments of the population and civil society. On September 8, 2025, civil society members, supported by local armed groups known as Wazalendo, organized a protest demanding his departure. The situation escalated when demonstrators attempted to enter the FARDC headquarters, resulting in casualties.

Civilian sources reported at least five deaths and twenty injuries during the Uvira protests. The FARDC confirmed the death of a 12-year-old child from a stray bullet and nine injuries, including four soldiers and five civilians. Gasita, of Banyamulenge origin, faced accusations of ties to Rwanda and the M23 rebel movement, though these claims were unsubstantiated. The protests occurred amid heightened ethnic tensions and anti-Tutsi rhetoric targeting the Banyamulenge community.

Why it mattersThe reassignment of General Gasita, who faced deadly protests and ethnic tensions in Uvira, affects military leadership and stability in eastern DRC.
