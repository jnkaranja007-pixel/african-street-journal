Gitega residents discover two bodies, raising concerns about public violence

Two new bodies were found in Gitega province on Monday, August twenty-fourth, including one man allegedly beaten to death by residents for stealing rabbits.

The bodies were discovered in two localities within the central Burundi province. One victim, Népomucène Ntakarutimana, was found in the Kiruhura valley on Kiremera hill, reportedly beaten to death by angry residents after being caught attempting to steal rabbits from Anitha Nshimirimana, according to Kiremera hill chief Jean Claude Mfukamensabe. Jacqueline Ndayishimiye, Anitha Nshimirimana, and Arsène Ciza have been arrested in connection with this incident and are being held for investigation.

On the same day, a male infant's body was discovered in the Karonga valley, which separates the Yoba and Rukoba neighborhoods. The Gitega police confirmed this discovery, but the circumstances of the baby's death and the identities of those responsible remain unknown. The infant was buried on site as investigations continue to determine the exact cause of death and identify any responsible parties.

These discoveries follow two other deaths in Bugendana commune on Tuesday, August twenty-fifth, under distinct circumstances. The head of the Bugendana police post has been arrested as part of the investigation into these events. These incidents have renewed concerns about violence, security force interventions, and the increase in vigilantism and acts of popular justice in the province.

Why it mattersResidents of Gitega are affected by increased violence and a lack of security, leading to concerns about public safety and the rule of law.

Burundian travelers face increased scrutiny and alleged extortion at Rwandan border

Burundian and Congolese travelers heading to Rwanda report intensified controls, repeated interrogations, and intimidation at border crossings, with some transporters allegedly.

Travelers from Burundi and Congo en route to Rwanda are experiencing heightened scrutiny and intimidation at border points, according to multiple testimonies collected by SOS Médias Burundi. Agents from the National Intelligence Service (SNR), supported by Imbonerakure youth league members, are reportedly conducting frequent checks and questioning individuals about their travel intentions to Rwanda.

Collective taxi drivers operating between Bujumbura and Kigali claim they are particularly targeted, facing accusations of transporting young people to the RED-Tabara armed group. Some drivers have been threatened, leading some transporters to change vehicles or suspend their services to avoid suspicion and harassment.

Transporters also allege they are being forced to pay significant sums of money to continue their activities. One driver in Muyinga stated that some transporters have paid up to one million Burundian francs to the police station in Muyinga to maintain their routes to Rwanda, indicating a potential racket affecting cross-border commerce.

Why it mattersBurundian and Congolese travelers and transporters face financial burdens and threats, impacting their ability to conduct cross-border business and travel to Rwanda.

Fire damages Kinindo market in Bujumbura, destroying goods and livelihoods

A fire on Tuesday ravaged a section of Kinindo market in southern Bujumbura, causing significant losses for several traders just days after another market fire in the city center.

A fire broke out around 10 o'clock in the Kinindo market, primarily affecting the mattress sales section. Firefighters, the Red Cross, and disaster management services responded quickly, bringing the flames under control within about an hour. However, a portion of the merchandise was destroyed, leaving many traders in shock over the loss of their goods and means of subsistence.

The incident follows a severe fire last Friday morning that devastated the Ndayizamba and Le Parisien commercial galleries in downtown Bujumbura. Finance Minister Alain Ndikumana visited the Kinindo site, stating that the cause of the fire is unknown and an investigation has begun. He assured that the government would monitor the situation and explore support measures for affected traders.

Antoine Muzaneza, president of the Association of Traders of Burundi (ACOBU), has called for concrete measures to enhance market security, including the creation of security committees and the implementation of controlled opening and closing hours. Traders are also requesting surveillance cameras to prevent future incidents and assist in identifying those involved in potential criminal acts, as attempts at theft occurred during the recent fire.

Why it mattersTraders in Bujumbura are directly affected by the destruction of their goods and livelihoods due to recurring market fires, impacting their ability to repay debts and resume business.

Burundi court imposes movement restrictions on provisional release

Individuals granted provisional release in Burundi must adhere to specific conditions, including restrictions on movement and contact, according to Iwacu.

Provisional release in Burundi now comes with strict conditions for the accused, including prohibitions on obstructing investigations or causing scandal through their conduct. They are also barred from visiting specific locations such as airports, ports, and train stations, or from being present there at designated times.

The accused must regularly report to the investigating magistrate or another official determined by the magistrate. They are also required to appear before the magistrate or judge whenever summoned and are forbidden from contacting victims or other specified individuals.

An order detailing these conditions is provided to the accused, who must sign it after notification. This notification of the provisional release order must occur within twenty-four hours, as reported by Iwacu.

Why it mattersIndividuals facing legal proceedings in Burundi are directly affected by these new conditions for provisional release, which restrict their freedom of movement and interaction.
