Burundi farmers face fertilizer shortage

Farmers in Gitega report insufficient fertilizer distribution and delays in refunds for previous orders.

Burundian farmers in Gitega are struggling with a severe fertilizer shortage, jeopardizing the A 2026 growing season. Many farmers have not received adequate fertilizer since 2020, with some receiving none at all. This scarcity threatens crop yields and household incomes.

Joseph Hatungimana, 49, from Kibiri, criticized the unequal distribution and difficulties in reclaiming money paid for past orders. He stated that instead of receiving fertilizer, farmers are given tickets to get refunds from microfinance and commercial banks. Joselyne Manirambona, 30, from Jimbi, noted that the 16 kg of fertilizer provided is insufficient for her needs, requiring at least two Imbura bags and one urea bag.

Evariste Gahungu, 72, from Rweza hill, is still awaiting documents to recover funds from the A 2025 season, having received only 16 kg of fertilizer for A 2026 when he needs three bags. Farmers are concerned about food shortages if production is insufficient, with some considering organic compost as an alternative. They are calling on the government to allow private importers to increase fertilizer supply.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burundi are experiencing fertilizer shortages that threaten their harvests and food security.

Burundi football league kicks off with regional rivalries

The 2026 football season opens with matches pitting Bujumbura against Muyinga and Vital'O FC against Messager Ngozi.

The Burundi football league commenced on Thursday, October 29, 2026, with a match between Inter Stars of Bujumbura and Olympic Star from Muyinga, a town in the far north of the country. The game took place at Stade Urunani.

Friday, October 30, featured two more opening day fixtures. Young Star FC played Panthère Noir FC at Ingoma stadium. Simultaneously, at Stade Urunani, Vital’O FC faced Messager Ngozi in what is described as a dramatic encounter.

The opening matches highlight the geographical and symbolic rivalries within Burundi's football landscape, connecting the capital city with distant provinces and presenting narratives of established clubs against emerging ones.

Why it mattersBurundian football fans across the country will follow the league's progress and the fortunes of their local teams.

Sport · 2026-10-30

Burundi team wins two matches in one day

The Hirondeaux beat Togo 4-3 and then Cape Verde 2-0 in Baku on Wednesday.

Burundi's Hirondeaux team faced Togo in a knockout match on Wednesday at 7:30 GMT, winning 4-3 after a back-and-forth game. Later that day, at 18:15 Baku time, they played Cape Verde at the Hovsan Stadium. The Burundian team adopted a compact and solid defensive strategy against the possession-focused Cape Verdeans.

The Hirondeaux secured a 2-0 victory against Cape Verde with two sharp counter-attacks. This followed a group stage where the team experienced mixed results, including initial losses to Portugal and Wales, followed by wins against Fiji and Bangladesh. Their final group match against Indonesia resulted in a significant 6-1 defeat.

This double victory on Wednesday advances the Hirondeaux in the tournament. The team demonstrated resilience after earlier losses and a heavy defeat in their final group game. Their performance against Togo, a team that had scored 13 goals in the first round, highlights their competitive spirit.
