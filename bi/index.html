Burundi farmers block road over fertiliser shortage

Residents of Kabanga barricaded the Rutana-Gihofi road with tree trunks to protest unequal fertiliser distribution.

Residents of Kabanga, Rutana province, blocked the Rutana-Gihofi road on October 30, 2026, using tree trunks to protest what they called unfair fertiliser distribution. The protest, organised by farmers who had not yet received chemical fertilisers from FOMI, an agricultural input company, temporarily halted traffic. Police and army units intervened to clear the road, which was only reopened after a fertiliser truck arrived.

The demonstration followed reports that neighbouring hills and surrounding areas had already received their fertiliser allocations. Farmers in Kabanga expressed anger that their hill had not been served. The situation escalated when local officials, including an agricultural monitor and a zone chief, were briefly held by angry residents before police intervention. Similar protests occurred on October 26 in Matongo, Butanyerera province, where residents blocked a road due to insufficient fertiliser supplies.

These repeated protests highlight tensions surrounding fertiliser distribution and farmers' difficulties accessing expected quantities. While farmers voice discontent, Burundi's authorities, speaking on national radio, have claimed widespread citizen satisfaction with the fertiliser distribution process. This official narrative contrasts with the protests recorded in Burunga and Butanyerera.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burundi are protesting the unequal distribution of essential fertilisers, impacting their ability to cultivate crops.

Agriculture · 2026-11-03

Burundian engineer creates maize sheller

Jean-Bosco Ndayizeye's company D-Techno built a machine to help farmers process harvests.

Jean-Bosco Ndayizeye, an engineer in Gitega, Burundi, co-founded Development Technology in 2023 to create useful equipment.

His company developed a maize sheller after observing farmers' difficulties in separating grains from cobs during large harvests. A local maize seed multiplier, who harvests 100 tonnes per season and was considering importing a sheller from China, has now turned to D-Techno for their machine.

Ndayizeye also previously participated in creating an irrigation machine in 2020 that uses water flow, not electricity or fuel, to pump water to fields.

Politics · 2026-11-03

Burundi commune discusses development projects amid challenges

Bubanza officials met with residents and students to review ongoing projects and local issues.

Officials in Bubanza, Burundi, held a public debate on October 24, 2026, at the local high school to discuss development projects for the 2026-2027 fiscal year. The meeting, supported by La Benevolencija Grands Lacs, involved the communal executive secretary Joachim Nduwumukama, council president Innocent Bikorimana, and Member of Parliament Sylvie Nizigama engaging with students and residents. Discussions covered priority projects, funding, execution progress, citizen participation, challenges, and future outlooks.

Joachim Nduwumukama reported that security is generally good across the eight zones, but noted five deaths in the past two months due to land disputes and witchcraft accusations. He also mentioned ongoing thefts from homes and fields. Nduwumukama highlighted that over 80% of Bubanza's population relies on agriculture and livestock, and expressed optimism about the current rainy season and planting for cultural season A. Communal revenue has increased due to an expanded administrative area encompassing former Musigati and Bubanza sectors.

Communal projects in Bubanza are funded through state budget-programmes, local revenues, and contributions from partners and natives of the area. The expanded administrative boundaries, now including Musigati, allow for tax collection over a larger territory. The secretary also confirmed that the rains have started, enabling farmers to begin planting for the current agricultural season.

Why it mattersResidents of Bubanza, Burundi, are directly affected by discussions on local development projects, land disputes, and security issues impacting their daily lives and livelihoods.
