Fire destroys part of Kinindo market, impacting mattress vendors

A new fire ravaged a section of the Kinindo market in Bujumbura on Tuesday, just days after another blaze hit commercial galleries downtown, causing significant losses for.

The fire, which started around 10 AM in the market's mattress section, was brought under control within an hour by firefighters, the Red Cross, and disaster management services. Despite rapid intervention, a portion of the goods was destroyed, leaving many traders in shock over the loss of their property and livelihoods, according to SOS Médias Burundi.

Finance Minister Alain Ndikumana visited the site and stated that the cause of the fire remains unknown, with an investigation underway. He assured affected traders that the government would monitor the situation and explore support measures, while also emphasizing the need for enhanced prevention in markets.

Antoine Muzaneza, president of the Association of Traders of Burundi (ACOBU), urged authorities to implement concrete security measures, including market security committees and surveillance cameras, to prevent future incidents and protect merchants' goods from theft attempts that occurred during the confusion.

Why it mattersMany traders lost their goods and means of subsistence, and the government is considering support measures for those affected.

Police actions lead to two deaths in Bugendana, Gitega province

Two individuals died in Bugendana, Gitega province, following separate incidents involving police, sparking calls for independent investigations from residents.

Désiré Nshimirimana, 25, died from a gunshot wound during a police intervention on Gaterama hill, according to SOS Medias Burundi. Police were reportedly at Joseph Bandora's home for arrests related to a land dispute when the intervention escalated, leading to a police officer firing a shot. Residents subsequently blocked the Bugendana-Kibimba road for nearly two hours.

Separately, Isaac Habonimana, 39, died on Gitongo hill after being severely beaten, with residents accusing Raphaël Nduwimana, the head of the Gitongo police post, of the assault. Dieudonné Bizimana, chief of Gitongo hill, stated Habonimana was accused of disturbing security in a bistro. Nduwimana has since been arrested and taken to police cells in Bugendana.

Following these events, six people were arrested on Gaterama hill and taken to police cells for investigation, as confirmed by Romuald Habonimana, chief of Gaterama hill. Residents in both Gaterama and Gitongo are demanding independent inquiries and the prosecution of the officers involved.

Why it mattersResidents in Bugendana are directly affected by the deaths of two community members and the subsequent arrests, leading to demands for justice and independent investigations.

Politics · 08/25/2026

M23 and Twirwaneho seize FARDC-Wazalendo positions in Bijombo

Clashes in Bijombo, South Kivu, led to the capture of several strategic locations previously held by FARDC and Wazalendo militias, according to local sources.

Fighters identified by local sources as affiliated with Twirwaneho and M23 engaged elements of the Congolese armed forces (FARDC) and Wazalendo militias in Bijombo, Uvira territory, South Kivu, on Sunday. The fighting resulted in the capture of positions in Rubarati, Ruhuha, and Malimba, with FARDC elements reportedly retreating to the Munanira mountains and Wazalendo militias moving towards Masango. Unconfirmed reports also suggest the involvement of Burundian Red Tabara fighters alongside Twirwaneho/M23.

The reported capture of Malimba, Ruhuha, and Rubarati holds strategic importance for controlling the Uvira highlands and key routes connecting several South Kivu localities. Local analysts suggest that control over these positions could facilitate movement towards Bukavu, Kaziba, Rurambo, and Minembwe, and offer access to the Rusizi plain and heights overlooking Uvira city.

These recent events follow a series of military movements in the Uvira and Fizi highlands since June 19, 2026, with several FARDC and Wazalendo positions, including Point Zero, Kakenge, Kalongi, and Kalingi, reportedly experiencing retreats. The ongoing conflict in eastern DRC has seen the AFC/M23 gain control of significant areas in North Kivu and South Kivu, while FARDC relies on Wazalendo militias to counter their advance.

Why it mattersThe conflict directly impacts the safety and stability of residents in the affected areas, potentially leading to displacement and disruption of daily life.

Jadot Nkurunziza mobilizes youth for environmental clean-up in Burundi

Jadot Nkurunziza, known as "Giti," organizes young people to remove water hyacinth from Lake Tanganyika and promote cleanliness in Ntahangwa commune.

Jadot Nkurunziza, born in 1994 in Nyakabiga, Bujumbura, has become a local figure for his environmental efforts. He spends time at a plant nursery by the Ntahangwa River and engages residents in the Ntahangwa commune on cleanliness. On Mwezi Gisabo Boulevard, he regularly educates young people about sanitation and planting flowers, according to a resident.

Nkurunziza also focuses on Lake Tanganyika, where water hyacinth sometimes threatens local activities and the ecosystem. He mobilizes other young people to remove these plants, an effort an inhabitant near the lake described as rare in their community. His vigilance extends to street waste, with residents reporting he asks people to pick up and properly dispose of plastic they discard.

Nkurunziza's commitment stems from his childhood, where his grandfather taught him the importance of trees for fruits, respiration, energy, and construction. After studying environmental protection in Uganda, he returned to Burundi to apply his training within his community, viewing trees as essential resources for daily life.

Why it mattersThis story matters to residents of Ntahangwa commune and those near Lake Tanganyika because it highlights local efforts to improve sanitation, remove invasive species, and promote environmental awareness directly impacting their daily lives and surroundings.
