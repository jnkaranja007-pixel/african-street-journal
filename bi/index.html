Nathan Ntahondi elected head of Burundi media regulator

The new president of the National Communication Council (CNC) promises to modernize the institution and foster dialogue with media professionals.

Nathan Ntahondi was unanimously elected president of Burundi's National Communication Council (CNC) on November 6, 2026, by the 15 members of the institution. He succeeds Espérance Ndayizeye, who had led the body since 2024. Léonce Ntakirutimana was elected vice-president, replacing the late Laurent Kaganda.

Ntahondi, a communications and ICT specialist formerly director general of Publications de presse burundaise (PPB) and chair of the ARCT, aims to reform the CNC to adapt to the digital media changes. He stated his commitment to making the CNC a modern institution that regulates through dialogue before sanctions and protects journalists.

Reporters Without Borders (RSF) ranked Burundi 119th out of 180 countries in its 2026 World Press Freedom Index, a slight improvement from the previous year. Ntahondi's election occurs as the country's media sector continues to face challenges to journalistic freedom.

Burundi football team faces Zambia in CAN qualifiers

The national team, known as Intamba Mu Rugamba, will play two matches in November 2026.

Burundi's national football team, the Intamba Mu Rugamba, is set to face Zambia in two matches for the Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers in November 2026. The first match will be played on November 13 in Ndola, Zambia, at the Levy Mwanawasa Stadium. The return leg is scheduled for November 17 in Bujumbura, Burundi.

Historically, Burundi has a balanced record against Zambia, with one win, three draws, and one loss in five previous encounters, conceding four goals and scoring four. Zambia's defense has recently been conceding an average of 2.2 goals per game, while Burundi's offense averages two goals per match. A draw in Ndola would be considered an exploit fondateur for Burundi.

These qualifiers represent a step up from the CECAFA tournament, marking a more serious challenge for the team. If the Intamba returns from Ndola standing, the November 17 match in Bujumbura will be a communion.

Why it mattersBurundian football fans and the national team will be directly affected by the outcome of these CAN qualifier matches.

Burundi football team loses six home matches

The national team has suffered five defeats at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium this season.

The Burundi national football team has lost six consecutive home matches at Levy Mwanawasa Stadium. For a long time, teams did not lose in Ndola, a law respected by all.

The team, known as the Chipolopolo, has lost five times at home during the 2025-2026 season. These defeats include a 0-2 loss to Morocco in September 2025.

A 0-1 loss to Tanzania in June 2024 during the World Cup 2026 qualifiers was initially dismissed as an accident. The team also drew 0-0 with Chad.

Why it mattersBurundi's national football team is directly affected by these repeated home losses.

Burundi sugar engineer missing, two bodies found

SOSUMO union leaders demand answers from management about the disappearance of Ismaël Mugisha.

Ismaël Mugisha, deputy head of agricultural equipment maintenance at the Moso Sugar Company (SOSUMO) in Burunga, Burundi, has been missing since October 30. His disappearance has caused significant concern within the company, prompting union leaders to request explanations from management. Two bodies have been discovered in the region within three days, though no link to Mugisha's case has been established.

Union officials sent a letter on November 3 to SOSUMO management, the industry minister, the governor of Burunga province, and security services, stating they have no reliable information on Mugisha's fate. Mugisha was last seen leaving home for work on October 30 and never arrived. The union expressed dissatisfaction with the lack of precise details regarding the search and the circumstances of his disappearance, calling it unprecedented for SOSUMO.

Searches are ongoing in the company's sugarcane plantations and along the Maragarazi and Mutsindonzi rivers, involving employees and police. The discovery of two bodies preceded these intensified searches. One body, identified as Thierry, a small trader from Gatandara, was found on November 3 in Makamba commune, reportedly decapitated and buried in a field.

Why it mattersThe disappearance of a key agricultural engineer at the state-owned sugar company and the discovery of two bodies create unease among SOSUMO employees and the local community.

Burundian diaspora celebrates culture in Canada

The tenth Ishaka cultural club performance took place at the National Arts Centre in Ottawa.

In October 2026, the Burundian diaspora in Ottawa and Gatineau, Canada, celebrated their culture through three key events. These included the tenth performance by the Ishaka cultural club at the National Arts Centre and an address by Burundian-Canadian Member of Parliament Arielle Kayabaga in the House of Commons. A conference on traditional values also took place at the University of Quebec in Outaouais.

MP Arielle Kayabaga highlighted Ishaka's tenth performance at the National Arts Centre during a session in the Canadian House of Commons, receiving applause from her colleagues. The events aimed to share Burundian cultural heritage with those living or born abroad. Preparations involved many Burundians in the Canadian capital, who were described as busy with the responsibility of universalizing their cultural legacy.

On October 1, an academic institution in Canada hosted a discussion on Burundian heritage and traditional values, attended by researchers, writers, students, and families. Participants discussed preserving traditional identity abroad amidst modernity. Young attendees shared their appreciation for values like respect and solidarity ('ugufashanya'), while organizers emphasized the role of parents in educating children about their heritage.

Why it mattersBurundian expatriates in Canada are actively engaged in cultural preservation and transmission to younger generations born abroad.
