Sport · 2026-10-31

Burundi U15 team loses to Canada in FIFA tournament

The Burundi U15 team was eliminated from the FIFA U15 World Cup Festival after a 7-0 loss to Canada in Division D quarter-finals.

The Burundi U15 team, nicknamed the "Hirondelles", was knocked out of the FIFA U15 World Cup Festival in Baku, Azerbaijan, on October 28. After winning their 16th and 8th round matches in Division D, the team suffered a 7-0 defeat against Canada in the quarter-finals. This result placed Burundi in the Top 8 of Division D, the tournament's fourth tier.

Burundi was placed in Group G with Portugal, Wales, Indonesia, Bangladesh, and Fiji. Following the group stage, the team was assigned to Level D, which ranks teams from 97th to 128th overall out of 189 participating nations. The "Hirondelles" had previously secured a 4-3 victory against Togo in the Round of 16 and a 2-1 win over Cape Verde in the Round of 8.

Spain was crowned the first-ever U15 world champion after defeating the Czech Republic in the final of Level A. The tournament featured 189 nations playing in an eight-a-side format. Eight African nations, including Algeria, Morocco, Angola, Cameroon, Guinea, Egypt, Ghana, and Kenya, topped their respective groups.

Agriculture · 2026-10-31

Burundi farmers face fertilizer shortages

Farmers in Gitega report insufficient fertilizer distribution and delays in refunds, threatening harvests.

Burundian farmers are struggling with a severe fertilizer shortage, leading to insufficient distribution and difficulties in reclaiming money paid for previous orders. In Gitega, the political capital, many farmers fear reduced production for the A 2026 growing season, which could impact household incomes and food security.

Joseph Hatungimana, 49, from Kibiri in Gitega commune, stated that since 2020, they have not received enough fertilizer, with some farmers receiving none at all. He also highlighted issues with obtaining refunds for past payments, receiving tickets instead of the fertilizer needed. Joselyne Manirambona, 30, from Jimbi, noted that the 16 kg of fertilizer provided is inadequate for her needs, requiring two bags of Imbura and one of urea for the A 2026 season.

Evariste Gahungu, 72, from Rweza hill, is still awaiting documents to recover funds from the A 2025 season, expecting five tickets for reimbursement. He received only 16 kg of fertilizer for the A 2026 season, while needing at least three bags. Manirambona plans to use organic compost to supplement, but fears it won't fully compensate for the lack of agricultural inputs, warning of potential food shortages for families.

Why it mattersBurundian farmers are experiencing fertilizer shortages that threaten their harvests, household incomes, and food security.
