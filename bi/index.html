Burundi demolishes market stalls, traders fear ruin

Ruyigi officials say stall demolitions prevent fires, but small business owners face lost income and debt.

Small traders in Burundi's Ruyigi market face ruin after authorities began demolishing their kiosks and selling points on September 16. The communal administration in Buhumuza province claims the operation aims to prevent fires and clear access for firefighters. Affected vendors, including palm oil, food, and fruit sellers, fear losing their primary income and being unable to repay loans taken for their businesses.

Traders reported having official authorization for their stalls and expressed surprise at the demolitions, stating they had recently spent capital on school expenses and restocking. Many had taken loans from local banks. They worry that relocation outside the market will reduce customers and income. Communal administrator Diane Niyibitanga stated the decision was made with trader representatives, citing fire risks and the need for clear access routes for emergency services, adding that traders were informed three months prior.

Niyibitanga warned that opposition to the demolitions could lead to sanctions, including reimbursement of demolition costs, administrative fines, or legal action. Local police are prepared to support the operation. The demolitions are impacting vendors of palm oil, cotton, food products, fruits, vegetables, and tailors using irons.

Burundi PARCEM accuses government of restricting fertilizer imports

The watchdog group fears new rules will favour a few privileged importers over fair market competition.

The Burundian organization PARCEM has denounced new government rules for importing chemical fertilizers, announced on October 21. PARCEM's national director, Faustin Ndikumana, stated that the measures could replace the current monopoly with a system favouring a select group of privileged importers, harming competition and investment. The Ministry of Environment, Agriculture, and Livestock outlined conditions including a letter to the ministry detailing the type, quantity, and origin of desired fertilizers, such as DAP and urea.

Ndikumana expressed concern on October 22 that the new system would not guarantee a genuine market opening, potentially perpetuating existing issues. Farmers in Gitega province are already reporting a lack of FOMI fertilizers for the current growing season, raising fears of reduced harvests and increased food insecurity. PARCEM questions the selection criteria, such as land size, and suggests experienced economic operators should also be allowed to import.

PARCEM warns that such a system could discourage investors and encourage corruption, advocating for transparent and objective import criteria accessible to all capable operators. Across Burundi, farmers report not receiving ordered fertilizers despite prior payments, with some alleging selective distribution favouring ruling party militants and associated traders. The ministry also extended the payment deadline for existing fertilizer stock until October 24, with distribution set to begin on October 26.

Why it mattersBurundian farmers face potential fertilizer shortages and increased costs due to government import restrictions.

Burundi marks 33rd anniversary of president Ndadaye assassination

A sober commemoration took place in Bujumbura on October 21, 2026, for the first democratically elected president.

Burundi observed the 33rd anniversary of the assassination of President Melchior Ndadaye and his collaborators on October 21, 2026. The commemoration occurred at the Place des martyrs de la démocratie in Bujumbura, with significant military and security presence. Attendees, including the president's widow, were searched upon entering the perimeter around the former 'Palais du 1er Novembre'.

The ceremony honored President Ndadaye, who was democratically elected, and the unknown martyr, with a red carpet laid to their tombs. Among the attendees were the widow of former president Pierre Nkurunziza, former president Sylvestre Ntibantunganya, the governor of Bujumbura, and the administrator of Mukaza. Government representation included the Minister of the Interior and the government's spokesperson.

High-ranking officials, including the President of the Senate and the President of the National Assembly, also attended the event. The President of the Republic arrived later, greeted by military and police officials lining the red carpet. This date marks 33 years since the 1993 assassination and subsequent large-scale killings.

Why it mattersBurundians are commemorating the assassination of their first democratically elected president and the subsequent violence.

Burundi official buried without autopsy amid death questions

Bruce Sebanza, an official at the Burundi Development Agency, died October 13, with family alleging torture.

Bruce Sebanza, an official at the Burundi Development Agency (ADB), died on October 13, 2026, at the institution's premises in Bujumbura. He was buried on Wednesday, October 21, without a public autopsy, prompting questions from observers. The National Independent Human Rights Commission (CNIDH) visited the ADB and requested investigations into the circumstances of his death.

At a service in Bujumbura, Sebanza's mother described his death as "barbaric torture." Relatives reported that Sebanza's body was transported to the Kamenge military hospital in a government pick-up truck on October 13. They stated the body was unclothed, wearing a soiled, bloodied singlet and underwear, with visible abrasions on his cheeks.

Witnesses reported hearing distress calls from the ADB premises during the night of October 12 to 13. A morgue worker allegedly mentioned physical marks on the body consistent with constriction, possibly by a rope. No independent medical conclusions on the cause of death have been released.

Why it mattersThe death of Bruce Sebanza, an official at the Burundi Development Agency, is being investigated due to allegations of torture and suspicious circumstances surrounding his burial without an autopsy.

Burundi central bank lost 2.4 million euros to hackers

Cybercriminals exploited fake invoices to transfer funds through Poland, China, and Hong Kong.

Burundi's central bank lost at least 2.4 million euros, equivalent to over 14.4 billion Burundian francs on the black market, in a cyberattack that began in 2024.

Good governance activist Gabriel Rufyiri of OLUCOME revealed the theft, which involved hackers using fake documents for equipment purchases and routing funds through Poland, China, and Hong Kong.

The central bank confirmed the cyberattack, stating hackers accessed a partner bank account to simulate a payment for Regideso equipment, highlighting vulnerabilities in the financial system.

Why it mattersBurundi's central bank lost millions of euros due to a cyberattack.
