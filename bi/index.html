Imbonerakure truck crash in Kabanga leaves 31 dead, sources say 75

President Évariste Ndayishimiye put the death toll at 31, while anonymous security sources and local ruling-party officials say at least 75 people were killed.

A truck carrying more than 150 members of the Imbonerakure, the youth league of Burundi's ruling CNDD-FDD party, ran into a crowd gathered at the Kabanga trading centre in Busoni commune, Butanyerera province, on Sunday, 15 November. President Évariste Ndayishimiye said on X on Monday that 31 citizens had died and several were injured, offering condolences to the families.

Anonymous security sources and local CNDD-FDD officials told SOS Médias Burundi that the toll was at least 75, a figure the outlet could not confirm independently. Witnesses accuse authorities and party representatives of rushing burials and preventing photographs, which they say was meant to limit the spread of information about the disaster.

According to local sources, the truck was returning from a paramilitary-style training on Mukinya hill, organised by party secretary-general Révérien Ndikuriyo, with new recruits on board. Some local officials instead say the young people were vulnerable people clearing trees for a government avocado model field, a claim local sources dispute.

Why it mattersFamilies of the dead and injured now face a disputed official toll, and the discrepancy leaves the true number of victims unconfirmed.

Burundi removes police general Ndababishije before his trial

President Évariste Ndayishimiye signed a decree on November 14 dismissing Romuald Ndababishije for corruption and passport breaches, while he is detained in Bujumbura.

Police Brigadier General Romuald Ndababishije was removed from the National Police of Burundi (PNB) by a presidential decree signed on November 14, 2026. The decree cites corruption and serious breaches of passport issuance rules, and it takes effect the day it was signed.

Ndababishije is held at Mpimba central prison in Bujumbura, after nearly three weeks in the cells of the National Intelligence Service (SNR). He was arrested on his return from a mission in Ethiopia, in circumstances that remain unclear, and was detained with three other police officers.

Sources close to his family say he and his co-accused are due to appear before a judge on November 26. Security sources told SOS Médias Burundi that an officer of his rank would not normally be dismissed before a court ruling and a court-martial, while other analysts argue disciplinary action can come first in some cases.

Why it mattersThe dismissal takes effect immediately, so Ndababishije no longer holds his police post while he awaits the November 26 hearing.

Burundi reforms Obuha to decentralize construction projects

A new decree will shift construction execution to communes and establish a director-general role, aiming to reduce currency pressure and improve project oversight.

The Office for Urbanism, Housing, and Construction (Obuha) in Burundi will soon undergo a reform through a new decree to address budget application difficulties. The reform will decentralize construction work to 11 communal departments, with Obuha responsible for quality verification. This change replaces the current structure of commissioners with a director-general working alongside communal departments, aiming to reduce pressure on foreign currency reserves.

Minister Ndikumana stated that the reform addresses irregularities in market attribution, where a single entity previously won large contracts and used funds for imports before work began. He cited the recent construction of judicial buildings as a success of decentralization, with funds released and communes now competing to execute projects. This decentralization is expected to alleviate pressure on central authorities and minimize expenses, with provinces receiving 500 million Burundian Francs (BIF) each for infrastructure rehabilitation.

The reform comes amid numerous unfinished or poorly executed projects, including zonal offices, rabbit breeding centers, and hospitals. In December 2025, Minister Ndikumana reported to Parliament that the government had disbursed 79 billion BIF for the construction of 360 zonal offices, highlighting past irregularities in the process. Obuha will no longer be overloaded with these responsibilities.

Why it mattersThe government has disbursed 79 billion BIF for 360 zonal offices, with a new decree set to decentralize construction to communes to improve oversight and reduce currency pressure.

Security · 2026-11-17

Burundi holds senior RTNB cameraman at intelligence service after fire

Bonaventure Niyongabo, a 60-year-old cameraman known as Gihuha, has been held since November 6 at the SNR in Bujumbura over a fire at the public broadcaster.

Bonaventure Niyongabo, a senior cameraman at Radio Télévision nationale du Burundi (RTNB), has been held since 6 November at the premises of the Service national de renseignement (SNR), the national intelligence service, in Bujumbura. He was arrested in a bar on Avenue du Large in the Mukaza district. Human rights organisations have reported arbitrary detention and mistreatment at the agency's cells.

Niyongabo, 60 and known as Gihuha, was reportedly questioned about a fire at the RTNB days earlier, which the broadcaster's managers had blamed on a short circuit. Three other staff members questioned in the same case, Yollande Niyubahwe, Claudette Butoyi and Prince Ntororwe, were released after questioning. His family is demanding his immediate release, and colleagues say he had no link to the fire and helped firefighters put it out.

Colleagues also say Niyongabo had criticised what they describe as discrimination against RTNB staff who are not members of the ruling party. One employee, speaking anonymously, described a hostile working environment for part of the staff. Colleagues say Niyongabo is approaching retirement age.

Why it mattersNiyongabo's family and colleagues are asking for his release so he can return to work before reaching retirement age.

Sport · 2026-11-17

Burundi league sees upsets as Vital'O FC falls

Vision FC, Never Give Up, Rukinzo, Inter Stars, Panthère Noire, and Musongati FC secured victories in a weekend of football action.

Football fans in Burundi witnessed a dramatic weekend of matches across the country. In Bujumbura, Vision FC defeated Muzinga FC 2-0. Meanwhile, in Ngozi, Never Give Up secured a 1-0 away win against Aigle Noir. Rukinzo also claimed a home victory, beating Bumamuru 3-1.

Sunday's fixtures included a close contest where Inter Stars narrowly beat Flambeau du Centre 1-0. Young Star and Olympic Star played to a goalless draw, ending 0-0. Kayanza United and Messager Ngozi shared the points in a 1-1 tie. The biggest upset occurred in Ngozi City, where Panthère Noire triumphed 3-1.

The weekend concluded with a significant result at home as Vital’O FC lost 0-2 to Musongati FC. These results from the twelfth matchday have reshuffled the league standings and provided talking points for supporters across Burundi.
