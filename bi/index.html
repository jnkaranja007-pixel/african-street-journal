Second mutilated body found near Makamba parish, fear grows

A second man discovered with violent injuries and genital mutilation near Saint-Joseph parish in less than two months has heightened local anxiety.

Residents of Kayogoro in Makamba province are gripped by fear following the discovery of a second mutilated body near the Saint-Joseph parish in under two months. The latest victim, a man from the Dunga area, was found early Thursday morning in a plantation belonging to the Catholic parish. Witnesses reported a head wound and his genitals tied with a rope. The absence of significant blood at the scene suggests the man may have been killed elsewhere and his body moved.

This discovery is particularly alarming due to its similarity to a previous incident. The first body, found near the same parish, also exhibited genital mutilation. This pattern has fueled unease among locals, who express confusion and fear over the repeated violence. "We don't understand what is happening. Two bodies found almost in the same place in such a short time, with similar mutilations, it's scary," said one resident anonymously.

The area of Kayogoro has been experiencing a concerning security situation. In July, the communal administrator of Makamba, Prosper Bizimana, noted several homicides in the region, including two individuals killed after being released by the justice system. These ongoing incidents raise questions about the local authorities' ability to control persistent violence in parts of the commune.

Why it mattersThe discovery of mutilated bodies in Kayogoro is causing fear among residents and raising concerns about local security and the authorities' ability to manage violence.

Burundi President Ndayishimiye Turns Youth League into "Commandos"

President Évariste Ndayishimiye announced that the Imbonerakure youth league members are now "commandos" and declared a desire for all Burundian citizens to be militarized.

During the closing ceremony of the 10th Imbonerakure Day in Gitega, President Évariste Ndayishimiye declared that the Imbonerakure, the youth league of the ruling CNDD-FDD party, would become "commandos." The event featured a parade of 119 Imbonerakure companies and included songs and slogans in Kirundi, with some translated as "We have all become commandos, Burundi is well guarded." The president stated his intention for "every Burundian citizen to be a soldier" and asserted that Burundi is well-protected against its friends and enemies.

Révérien Ndikuriyo, the CNDD-FDD's secretary general, pledged to strengthen the Imbonerakure and urged them to contribute to the nation's development vision for an emergent Burundi by 2040 and a developed Burundi by 2060. He also called for vigilance against the country's enemies, whom he believes have not disarmed. Participants sang about past difficulties and recent changes since the CNDD-FDD came to power, noting improvements in education, child-rearing, and peace.

The president expressed satisfaction that the youth are no longer swayed by opposition rhetoric, referencing events from 2015. He emphasized that while taking action might be one thing, knowing how to execute it was another. The Imbonerakure Day celebrations, held at the Ingoma stadium, underscored the party's support for its leadership and its commitment to national defense.

Why it mattersThe transformation of the Imbonerakure into "commandos" and the call for all citizens to be militarized directly impacts the political landscape and security of Burundi.
