Burundi detains RTNB cameraman over media house fire

Bonaventure Niyongabo, 60, held by intelligence services since November 6 amid questions about a fire at the public broadcaster.

Bonaventure Niyongabo, a senior cameraman at Burundi's national broadcaster RTNB, has been detained by the National Intelligence Service (SNR) since November 6. Niyongabo, known as "Gihuha," was arrested in a bar in Bujumbura and taken to SNR headquarters. His detention has raised concerns among colleagues and family, who say he has no link to a recent fire at the RTNB. The fire, which occurred days before his arrest, was initially attributed to a short circuit by RTNB officials.

Niyongabo, aged 60, is reportedly being questioned about the RTNB fire. Three other RTNB staff members, Yollande Niyubahwe, Claudette Butoyi, and Prince Ntororwe, were questioned in connection with the incident but have since been released. Niyongabo's colleagues told SOS Médias Burundi that he participated in efforts to extinguish the fire alongside military personnel and question the reasons for his continued detention. His family is demanding his immediate release.

Some RTNB staff members have anonymously reported discriminatory practices within the media house, alleging that certain employees are sidelined due to their perceived opposition to the ruling party. One anonymous staff member described working in a hostile environment. Niyongabo's colleagues are calling for his release so he can resume his professional activities as he nears retirement age and for fair treatment of all workers at RTNB.

Why it mattersThe detention of a senior journalist and allegations of discrimination at the state media house raise concerns about press freedom and fair employment practices in Burundi.

Bonesha FM launches 45-day appeal for 500 million Burundian francs

The independent radio station, silenced for nearly six years after the 2015 political crisis, says it faces financial suffocation.

Bonesha FM, an independent radio station in Burundi, has launched a 45-day fundraising campaign aiming to raise 500 million Burundian francs. The campaign was officially launched on Friday, October 30, by station director Léon Masengo, after the station was silenced for nearly six years following the 2015 political crisis.

Masengo has appealed to citizens, organisations, embassies, partners, the diaspora and the station's friends to make a financial contribution or offer other support. He said the station needs the funds to keep its broadcasts and services running, and promised to report publicly on the sums raised and how they are used.

The station was founded on February 19, 1996, as Radio Umwizero, meaning "Hope" in Kirundi, and changed its name to Radio Sans Frontières Bonesha FM on January 29, 2001. Masengo said the station's programmes on public information, citizen debate and community issues are at stake.

Burundi Senate president targets police officers

Gitega: Senate President Gervais Ndirakobuca criticizes judicial police officers for overstepping their legal authority.

Gitega: Senate President Gervais Ndirakobuca stated on October 29, 2026, that some judicial police officers (OPJ) are acting with impunity, disregarding laws and overstepping their authority. He explained that OPJs are wrongly classifying cases as closed, a power reserved for prosecutors, and are even using their positions to recover civil debts through coercion and bribery.

Ndirakobuca urged the Minister of the Interior, Léonidas Ndaruzaniye, to transfer OPJs who are not performing their duties correctly, citing public fear over their abuses and the need to break up established networks. He also highlighted instances where OPJs ignore their superiors, leading to a lack of hierarchical control in some communes.

The Senate President announced plans to collaborate with the public to set traps for corrupt OPJs as part of the National Anti-Corruption Strategy. He also noted that some transferred OPJs manage to return to lucrative posts shortly after reassignment, indicating a need for stronger oversight and accountability within the police force.

Why it mattersBurundian citizens are directly affected by the alleged abuses and corruption of judicial police officers, including unlawful detention and debt recovery.

Burundian parents run school canteen for Congolese refugee children

Burundian and Congolese parents cook daily lunches at Musenyi camp school, supported by the World Food Programme, to draw refugee children into class.

At the Congolese refugee camp of Musenyi in Burundi, Burundian parents living near the camp and Congolese parents have started a school canteen at the Ecofo Gatereri school to get refugee children to attend lessons. The World Food Programme supplies rice, beans, flour and oil for the meals.

The cooking starts in the morning, with parents bringing firewood and mixing the flour with minerals and vitamins to boost its nutritional value. Lunch is ready when classes end around midday, and children eat maize paste and beans at their desks, which are turned into tables. Some teachers say the water for handwashing is often short.

Children who are not enrolled or too young for school also crowd around the canteen, crying that they are hungry. Some slip past teachers, while others are turned away because they are not on the teachers' class lists.

Why it mattersRefugee children who are not on school lists are left out of the meals and sent away from the canteen.
