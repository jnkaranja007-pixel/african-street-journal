Burundi police hold 21 residents over fertiliser protest

At least 21 people, including four women and three infants, remain in custody after a dispute over fertiliser distribution in Gitega province.

At least 21 residents of Rango hill, in the Murago zone of Nyabihanga commune in Gitega province, central Burundi, remain detained after protesting the distribution of insufficient quantities of chemical fertiliser. The first arrests came on Saturday 31 October, when Léa Nijimbere, the ruling CNDD-FDD party's representative on the hill, and Bernard Barihinyuza, the hill chief, were taken into custody. 16 more residents were arrested on Friday 6 November.

Residents say households paid for fertiliser expecting 16 kilograms each, but some received less. The protest turned into confrontations with local officials, and stones broke windows at the zone office. Local sources say members of the Imbonerakure, the youth league of the ruling party, took part in the arrests, which the detainees are holding at the commune jail. The Association for the Fight Against Unemployment and Torture (ALUCHOTO) says the arrests were irregular and demands the unconditional release of everyone detained.

ALUCHOTO's coordinator, Vianney Ndayisaba, said by phone on Thursday 12 November that at least 21 people, including four women and three infants, were still held. Similar fertiliser protests were reported in Busangana, in Matongo commune, on 26 October, and in Kabanga, in Rutana commune, on 30 October, where roads were blocked until a fertiliser truck arrived.

Why it mattersHouseholds that paid for fertiliser and received less are facing detention, and three infants are being held with their mothers at the commune jail.

Burundi arrests prison director and two officials over businessman's privileges

The three Mpimba prison officials are held by the National Intelligence Service (SNR), accused of granting special treatment to a businessman linked to trade in M23-controlled.

Colonel Jimmy Ndayishimiye, director of the central prison in Bujumbura known as Mpimba, and two other prison officials are in custody of the National Intelligence Service (SNR). They are suspected of granting privileges to businessman Aloys Ntakarutimana, known as Wakenya, who is accused of running commercial activities in a zone held by the M23 armed movement in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC).

Ndayishimiye and Spès Caritas Shurweryimana, the officer in charge of social affairs, were arrested on Thursday morning after being summoned to SNR headquarters in Bujumbura. A police major responsible for prison police was arrested on Wednesday evening, the same night Ntakarutimana was detained. Sources told SOS Médias Burundi that Ntakarutimana had earlier spent several weeks in SNR cells before his transfer to Mpimba on April 7.

The officials are suspected of easing his trips outside the prison and of allowing him a private case he had built inside the facility, where he spent part of his evenings. Sources say rivalries and denunciations inside the prison may have drawn intelligence attention to these advantages. The information has not yet been verified by SOS Médias Burundi.

Why it mattersPrison staff who allowed the businessman outings and a private case now face detention, and the case shows how inmate privileges can reach the intelligence services.

Business · 2026-11-13

Burundi city disrupted by blaring advertisements

Loud, repetitive advertising panels in Gitega are causing economic losses and health concerns for residents and businesses.

Loud, repetitive advertising panels in Gitega are disrupting daily life, with constant noise from 7 a.m. onwards. Businesses and residents near the Idrecom site in the city center are subjected to blaring sound spots for up to three months straight.

The incessant noise makes normal conversation difficult and is driving away customers. Taxi-moto drivers report losing fares because clients cannot hear them, and hotel managers say guests complain about the lack of quiet. Shops and boutiques are also seeing fewer visitors who cut their visits short due to the disturbance.

Beyond the economic impact, residents worry about potential health problems from prolonged exposure to high decibels. A local doctor warned that such noise pollution can lead to chronic stress, sleep disturbances, hearing damage, and fatigue, with one mother expressing fear that the noise could make people sick.

Why it mattersBusinesses near the Idrecom advertising site in Gitega are losing customers and income due to constant noise pollution.

Sport · 2026-11-13

Nsekera brings light to Burundi's sports federations

Lydia Nsekera has transformed opaque sports associations into accountable entities across all of Burundi's communes.

Lydia Nsekera, appointed to a ministerial role, has begun transforming Burundi's sports sector from a labyrinth of corruption into a functional system. Previously, sports federations operated in near-religious opacity, with budgets disappearing and promises yielding silence.

Nsekera's approach focused on work rather than promises, bringing light to previously shadowed operations. Sports associations are now beginning to be transparent, with each commune in Burundi, from Kirundo to Makamba, establishing its own sports section that reports and is accountable.

This change means that youth in every commune, including Ngozi and Bururi, are now recognized and have opportunities to participate and dream in sports, marking a silent revolution in the nation's sporting landscape.

Why it mattersYouth in every commune now have sports opportunities and recognition, a change driven by Lydia Nsekera's reforms.
