Bonesha FM seeks 500 million francs to avoid closure

The independent radio station launched a 45-day fundraising campaign on Friday, October 30, to cover its financial shortfalls.

Bonesha FM, an independent radio station in Burundi, is facing financial collapse and has launched a 45-day fundraising campaign to raise 500 million Burundian francs. The station's director, Léon Masengo, announced the appeal on Friday, October 30, stating the funds are necessary to prevent the media outlet from ceasing operations.

Masengo called on citizens, organizations, embassies, partners, the diaspora, and friends of the station to contribute financially or offer other forms of support. He emphasized the radio's vital role in public information, citizen debate, and community programs, noting that economic difficulties are hindering its ability to broadcast regularly. Masengo also pledged transparent communication regarding the collected funds and their usage.

Bonesha FM, originally established as Radio Umwizero in 1996, was renamed in 2001 and has been a significant voice in Burundi's media landscape. The current financial drive aims to secure the station's future and maintain an independent media space, with Masengo stating, "A radio that informs needs a community that supports it."

Why it mattersBonesha FM's financial struggles threaten its ability to provide information and community programs to Burundian citizens.

Woman and infant found dead in Gitega

Police arrested three people in connection with the woman's death and summoned two others regarding the infant.

Two bodies were discovered on November 5 and 6, 2026, in Gitega province, Burundi. The victims were a 33-year-old woman and a newborn infant. Authorities are investigating both deaths, which occurred in separate communes.

Marie Rose Nkurunziza, 33, was found dead on November 6 in Bugendana commune, with evidence suggesting she was killed by a sharp weapon. Local administrator Euphrasie Niyimbona stated the motive is unclear, but a refused abortion is being considered. Three individuals, Tharcisse, Moussa, and Pascal, were arrested and detained by police. The previous day, November 5, a newborn was discovered in Gishubi commune, believed to have been abandoned by its mother.

The infant's case involves Daphrose Niyonzima and her daughter Aline Nizigiyimana, who have been summoned to appear before police on November 9. These discoveries follow other recent deaths with disputed circumstances in the province, including a man found hanged on October 27, where residents questioned the suicide theory.

Why it mattersThe deaths of a woman and a newborn, with arrests and summonses issued, directly affect the local communities in Gitega province.

Burundian youth learns sewing after exam failure

Vanessa Munezero, from Muhanga, chose tailoring after failing state exams to start a new career.

Vanessa Munezero, born in 1996 in Muhanga, chose to learn tailoring after failing her state examination, which prevented her from pursuing further studies. Despite initial feelings of underestimation from peers in other sections, Munezero persisted with her training at Lycée technique secondaire Buntu in Buye. She now teaches her skills to other young people, inspiring them to learn a trade.

A neighbor observed that other young women seeking work in Arab countries as domestic workers could approach Munezero to learn sewing, noting her inspirational influence. Munezero's decision to pursue tailoring came after her failure in the state exam at the 9th fundamental year, a setback that led some classmates to seek work abroad or marry.

The director of the technical school encouraged students in the tailoring section to continue their studies and acquire sewing machines. Munezero recalled that even though others underestimated them, her section was responsible for sewing the uniforms for the entire school.

Why it mattersVanessa Munezero's story shows how young Burundians can learn a trade like sewing after failing academic exams.
