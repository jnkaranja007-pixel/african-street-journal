boy killed by stray bullet near Burundi military range

A 13-year-old Congolese refugee died and another was injured by an exploding bullet while cooking maize at a transit center.

A 13-year-old boy from a Congolese refugee family died Saturday from an exploding bullet near a military firing range in Cishemere, Bujumbura province. The boy was killed while preparing to grill maize at the Cishemere transit center when a bullet ignited from the heat of the fire. Another child was injured in the incident, raising new safety concerns for asylum seekers at the site.

Witnesses reported the children were gathered around a fire when the abandoned bullet exploded. The victim, originally from Luvungi in eastern Congo's South Kivu region, died at the scene. The injured child received medical attention. The Cishemere transit center houses Congolese asylum seekers awaiting transfer to refugee camps, and its proximity to the military range worries residents about unexploded ordnance.

A refugee at the center, who asked to remain anonymous, urged Burundian authorities to secure the area and protect asylum seekers. Humanitarian sources indicate that some asylum seekers from Cishemere and Makombe are expected to be transferred to Busuma, raising concerns about their reception conditions given the risks refugees face at reception sites.

Why it mattersCongolese refugees in Burundi are at risk of death from unexploded ordnance near military sites.

Burundi court receives accounts from only 12 of 303 entities

The Court of Audit president reported that most public entities failed to submit their 2025-2026 financial accounts.

Only 12 out of 303 public entities submitted their financial accounts for the 2025-2026 fiscal year to Burundi's Court of Audit. Léopold Kabura, president of the Court of Audit, presented this concerning finding to the National Assembly on October 15, 2026.

Among the 38 central administration entities, administrative public establishments, and personalized state administrations required to report, only four complied. These included the Ministry of Finance, the National Office for Pensions and Professional Risks (ONPR), the National Blood Transfusion Centre (CNTS), and the National Institute of Statistics of Burundi (INSBU).

Publicly held companies, including several banks like the Bank of the Republic of Burundi (BRB) and Burundi Airlines, along with 35 public investment projects, also failed to present their accounts. The Court of Audit's report highlights a lack of transparency in public fund management.

Why it mattersBurundians are affected by the lack of transparency in public fund management by state institutions.

Business · 2026-10-26

Burundi investor launches solar car charging station

Electric vehicles offer Burundi economic and environmental benefits by reducing fuel imports and emissions.

Electric vehicles are increasingly seen in Burundi, offering economic advantages by reducing the need for imported fuel and saving the country's foreign currency. Rémy Marie Nkurunziza stated that these vehicles do not pollute the atmosphere and produce zero greenhouse gas emissions, unlike fossil fuel cars that release carbon dioxide.

The shift to electric cars also presents energy benefits, as Burundi can utilize its own abundant solar and hydroelectric power sources instead of importing energy. An investor has already established a solar-powered charging station in Bujumbura, an initiative Nkurunziza described as positive and encouraging for the region's clean energy transition.

Nkurunziza believes that the installation of charging stations can help Burundi reduce its dependence on traditional fuels in the medium term. He also emphasized that importing new technology requires importing the necessary skills to operate and maintain it.

Why it mattersBurundians will benefit from reduced fuel costs and cleaner air due to the adoption of electric vehicles.
