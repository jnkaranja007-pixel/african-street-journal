Burundi Election Authority Adds Over One Million Voters

The National Independent Electoral Commission is accelerating preparations for the 2027 presidential election by enrolling new voters.

More than one million new voters are expected to be added to Burundi's electoral roll for the 2027 presidential election. The National Independent Electoral Commission (CENI) announced the partial voter registration operation is scheduled for October 2026. This initiative aims to include Burundians who were not yet 18 during the 2025 registration, recent returnees, and others who missed the previous enrollment.

The registration will take place in over four thousand centers, which will also serve as polling stations. The CENI president, Prosper Ntahorwamiye, stated that the goal is to ensure all eligible citizens can participate in the 2027 election. October is also slated for accrediting political party and candidate representatives, though exact dates are not yet finalized. The CENI is holding regular meetings with electoral partners to address potential challenges.

The CENI also addressed concerns about lost voter cards, assuring that citizens listed in the electoral database and meeting requirements will not be disenfranchised. Measures will be taken to allow them to vote. Ntahorwamiye emphasized the CENI's commitment to transparency in auditing the electoral roll, noting that public display of lists is a key verification step. He acknowledged that human work is always improvable and welcomes feedback.

Why it mattersBurundian citizens eligible to vote will be able to participate in the 2027 presidential election through this new voter registration drive.

Burundi Lakes Face Pollution, Overfishing Despite Economic Potential

Experts highlight the significant economic and freshwater resources of Burundi's lakes, warning of threats from pollution and unsustainable practices.

Burundi's lakes, including Lake Tanganyika which holds sixteen percent of the world's freshwater reserves, offer substantial opportunities for agriculture, fishing, tourism, and transport. Dr. Jean Népomuscène Sindayihebura noted that Lake Tanganyika alone provides nine percent of these global reserves, with the capacity to supply one hundred percent of potable water to several cities. The fishing sector yields between seventeen thousand and twenty-two thousand tons of fish annually, equating to nearly two kilograms per Burundian.

Despite their importance, these vital water bodies face severe challenges. Pollution from plastic waste, household wastewater, and industrial and agricultural chemicals is rampant. Overfishing also strains the aquatic resources. In twenty twenty-five, Burundi imported over eleven thousand tons of oils, greases, and oil waste, much of which ends up in Lake Tanganyika, either directly or indirectly. Industries along the lake and pastures near the Rusizi River also contribute undocumented pollutants.

These issues were discussed at a public conference organized by the NGO La Benevolencjia and its media partners, under the theme 'The Lakes of Burundi: Opportunities, Challenges, and Missed Opportunities.' The event aimed to raise awareness among decision-makers, the public, academics, and civil society to promote the preservation, restoration, and sustainable management of these critical resources.

Why it mattersBurundi's lakes are crucial for economic activities like fishing and agriculture, but pollution and overfishing threaten these livelihoods and the potential for economic development.

Burundi Refugee Parents Struggle to Buy School Supplies Amid Aid Cuts

Families in Musasa camp face difficult choices between food and education as humanitarian aid decreases and living costs rise.

Parents in Burundi's Musasa refugee camp are expressing deep concern as the September 7 school year opening approaches. Many report significant challenges in acquiring essential school supplies for their children, a situation exacerbated by reduced humanitarian assistance and increased living expenses. Historically, refugee students received school kits through humanitarian aid, often from UNHCR via the Jesuit Refugee Service, but these distributions have faced delays in recent years due to funding issues, leading to parental fears of a repeat.

The financial strain is particularly acute for larger families. One parent, Oumar, who has six children, highlighted the difficulty of affording basic necessities like notebooks and pens when food is already a primary concern. He noted that decreased humanitarian aid and rising market prices make it nearly impossible to prioritize school supplies. "We must choose between feeding our children and buying their supplies," he stated, emphasizing the need for assistance to enable their education.

Beyond supplies, the departure of Burundian teachers from the camp during the 2025-2026 school year due to insufficient pay has also caused disruptions. Parents fear that these teaching gaps will continue, impacting their children's already challenging educational journey. This exodus of educators has led to periods where classes were suspended, raising anxieties about the upcoming academic year and the continuity of education for the refugee children.

Why it mattersParents in the Musasa refugee camp are affected by reduced humanitarian aid and rising costs, forcing them to choose between buying food and school supplies for their children.

Burundi troops fight M23 rebels in eastern Congo

Violent clashes involving Burundian soldiers and Congolese forces against M23 and allied groups are ongoing in several territories of South Kivu province.

Heavy fighting has erupted in multiple areas across the Fizi, Uvira, and Mwenga territories of South Kivu province in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The clashes pit the Congolese army (FARDC), supported by Burundi's National Defence Force (FDNB) and Kinshasa-backed Wazalendo militias, against fighters from the AFC/M23 and Twirwaneho groups. Local sources reported the use of drones and heavy weaponry by forces allied with the FARDC in areas like Malimba, Rubarati, and Ruhuha.

A Wazalendo combatant claimed that FARDC, FDNB, and Wazalendo forces recaptured positions previously held by AFC/M23, Twirwaneho, and Red Tabara in Malimba, Ruhuha, and Rubarati. However, Twirwaneho's spokesperson stated their fighters repelled attacks in other locations, including Kabanju, Kipupu, Nyamabuguma, and Point Zéro, and that pro-Kinshasa forces retreated. These claims could not be independently verified.

The Minembwe region is a focal point, with FARDC, FDNB, and Wazalendo forces reportedly aiming to secure positions around Minembwe to prevent AFC/M23 advances towards Tanganyika province. Operations also target Bijombo to halt the progress of AFC/M23 and Twirwaneho fighters towards Uvira and control key routes. An internal Congolese report from December 2025 indicated over twenty-nine thousand Burundian soldiers were deployed in DRC between August 2022 and December 2025, with about ten thousand remaining at the end of 2025.

Why it mattersBurundian soldiers are engaged in combat operations in eastern Congo, affecting regional stability and potentially drawing the country further into the conflict.

Burundi Football Stadiums Fall Silent for International Competitions

Seven major stadiums across Burundi will suspend league play throughout September as national teams compete in continental and regional tournaments.

Burundi's top football league, the Primus Ligue, is temporarily halting all matches, affecting seven key stadiums including Stade Intwari in Bujumbura and Stade Ingoma in Gitega. This nationwide pause is in effect for September 2026 to allow national teams to participate in crucial international fixtures. The decision means stadiums will be empty of club crowds as the focus shifts to the country's continental and regional sporting commitments.

The suspension is a direct response to the Confederation of African Football (CAF) Interclub competitions and the CECAFA qualifying tournament, alongside the CAN 'Pamoja KEN-TAN-UGA' tournament. Burundi's U-20 and senior national teams, known as the Intamba, are expected to represent the nation in these events. Clubs like Aigle Noir CS and Rukinzo FC will also be competing on the continent.

The Competition Organisation Commission, led by president Yussuf Mossi, has stated that the Primus Ligue will resume immediately after the CAF Interclub preliminary return legs, scheduled for September 11-13. A new official calendar will be released subsequently. For now, the pitches will rest, and attention will be directed towards the national teams representing Burundi abroad.

Why it mattersFootball clubs and their associated businesses will experience a temporary halt in operations and revenue generation due to the league suspension.
