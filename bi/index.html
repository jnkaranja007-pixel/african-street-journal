Burundi farmers face fertilizer shortage

Farmers in Gitega report insufficient fertilizer distribution and delays in refunds for previous orders.

Burundian farmers in Gitega are struggling with a severe fertilizer shortage, jeopardizing the A 2026 growing season. Many farmers have not received adequate fertilizer since 2020, with some receiving none at all. This scarcity threatens crop yields and household incomes.

Joseph Hatungimana, 49, from Kibiri, criticized the unequal distribution and difficulties in reclaiming money paid for past orders. He stated that instead of receiving fertilizer, farmers are given tickets to get refunds from microfinance and commercial banks. Joselyne Manirambona, 30, from Jimbi, noted that the 16 kg of fertilizer provided is insufficient for her needs, requiring at least two Imbura bags and one urea bag.

Evariste Gahungu, 72, from Rweza hill, is still awaiting documents to recover funds from the A 2025 season, having received only 16 kg of fertilizer for A 2026 when he needs three bags. Farmers are concerned about food shortages if production is insufficient, with some considering organic compost as an alternative. They are calling on the government to allow private importers to increase fertilizer supply.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burundi are experiencing fertilizer shortages that threaten their harvests and food security.

Burundi U15 team advances to knockout stage

The Burundi U15 team secured two wins in the group stage of the Bakou 2026 festival, advancing to face Togo.

The Burundi U15 team has qualified for the Bakou 2026 festival, a FIFA event held in Baku from October 24 to October 30, 2026. The team was directly invited by FIFA as part of its global development program, recognizing the work of the Football Federation of Burundi in youth training. Burundi competed in Group G, facing Portugal, Wales, Fiji, Bangladesh, and Indonesia.

In their matches, Burundi lost narrowly to Portugal 0-1 and to Wales 1-3, but secured victories against Fiji and Bangladesh with identical 2-0 scorelines. Their final group match on October 26 resulted in a 6-1 defeat against Indonesia. Despite the loss, the team's performance earned them a place in the next round.

The Burundi U15 team will now play Togo in a round of 16 match on October 28. This advancement continues their journey in the festival, which was designed by FIFA as a celebration of youth football rather than a traditional elimination tournament.

Why it mattersYoung Burundian footballers are participating in an international festival and have advanced to the knockout stages.

Burundi football league kicks off with regional rivalries

The 2026 football season opened with matches pitting clubs from different regions against each other.

The Burundi football league began on Thursday, October 29, with Inter Stars from Bujumbura playing Olympic Star from Muyinga at Stade Urunani.

The opening match represented a clash between the capital city club, Inter Stars, and Olympic Star, a team travelling from the far north of the country.

The league continued on Friday, October 30, with Young Star FC facing Panthère Noir FC at Ingoma, and Vital’O FC playing Messager Ngozi at Stade Urunani.

Why it mattersBurundians across the country will follow the football league matches involving teams from their regions.

Burundi footballer Yussuf Ndayishimiye rises from obscurity

The OGC Nice player was named among Ligue 1's top 11 African players in April 2024.

Yussuf Ndayishimiye, born October 27, 1998, began his football journey at Saint-Augustin at age nine, facing early rejection from PVP. He then joined Mukaza in the third division, developing his skills in obscurity before moving to Les Lierres in the second division.

After being overlooked for six matches, Ndayishimiye was given 20 minutes in his seventh game for Les Lierres, an opportunity he seized to secure a permanent place in the starting 11. His performance caught the attention of Révérien Ndikuriyo, leading to a move to Aigle Noir in the 2016-2017 season.

Ndayishimiye's career advanced with stints in Turkey at Yeni Malatyaspor from 2019 to 2021, followed by a move to Istanbul Basaksehir. He then joined OGC Nice in France, where he has established himself and was recognized in April 2024 as one of Ligue 1's 11 best African players.

Why it mattersBurundian footballer Yussuf Ndayishimiye's career progression from local clubs to OGC Nice and recognition among top African players in France affects national pride and football aspirations.
