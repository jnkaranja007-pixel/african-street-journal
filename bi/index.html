Burundi banks struggle with cash shortages and long queues

Customers face rationed withdrawals and delayed transfers, eroding trust in the banking system.

Burundians are experiencing significant difficulties accessing their money from banks, with long queues, rationed withdrawals, and unavailable liquidity becoming common. Customers report waiting for hours, witnessing what they describe as preferential treatment for some clients, while others face refusals to withdraw their own funds due to a lack of cash, even in local currency.

Beyond withdrawal issues, bank transfers are reportedly delayed for weeks due to inadequate systems or processing failures. This situation directly impacts clients, leading to doubts about their solvency, tarnished reputations, and employees waiting for salaries that have already been transferred. The banks' inability to execute basic transactions and return funds to customers undermines their credibility.

While cultural habits of saving at home are often cited as reasons for distrust, the current banking system's failures make this practice a rational risk management strategy. The promises of security, availability, and fair treatment are compromised by endless queues, nepotism, cash shortages, and withdrawal limits, leading many to keep their money at home for certainty, time, peace, and dignity.

Why it mattersBurundians are unable to access their own money reliably, impacting their daily lives and financial stability.

Kirundo residents go months with little power as shortages spread

Residents of Kirundo, in northern Burundi, say power has been almost absent for about three months, forcing families and shops to rely on solar panels and torches.

Kirundo, a town in northern Burundi, has faced persistent electricity shortages for several months. The outages have disrupted daily life, hurt commercial activity and complicated the work of several establishments in the town.

One resident said he has had almost no electricity for about three months, and that when power does return it lasts only a very short time. A worker at a guest house said locals have grown used to living without it, and that when it comes back it feels almost like a miracle. Milk sellers and other merchants say their businesses have slowed to a standstill, and that the cold storage of some products has become difficult.

Shortages have also been reported in several other regions, including Bujumbura, the economic capital, where many shops, government offices and United Nations agencies are based. Residents and traders in Kirundo are calling on the authorities and the national water and electricity utility, REGIDESO, to take urgent steps to restore a regular supply.

Why it mattersMilk sellers and other small merchants in Kirundo, whose products need refrigeration and whose equipment depends on power, say the outages are halting their sales.

UPRONA influential member found buried in field

Venant Nyandwi was discovered on November 10 in Mwaro province, bound and killed.

Venant Nyandwi, an influential UPRONA member in Kayokwe, was found dead and buried in a sweet potato field on Kibogoye hill, Mwaro province, on November 10. The 65-year-old was reportedly tied up before being killed by unidentified individuals. His body was exhumed by residents and taken to Kibumbu Regional Hospital morgue.

According to Côme Gahungu, the Kibogoye hill chief, Nyandwi was violently bound on the night of November 9 as he returned from a bar. The exact circumstances and motives for his death remain unknown, though Gahungu suggested it could be a settling of scores. No suspects have been arrested.

This death occurs as over 100 bodies have been recorded in Gitega province since the start of 2026, many under unclear circumstances. Residents are calling for independent investigations to identify those responsible for Nyandwi's death and bring them to justice.

Why it mattersThe death of an influential political figure and the high number of unexplained deaths in Gitega province raise security concerns for residents.

11 Burundian parties back Ndayishimiye for 2027

The parties, which won no seats in the 2025 legislative elections, pledged to pool resources for a first-round victory.

11 Burundian political parties officially announced their support for Évariste Ndayishimiye, the CNDD-FDD candidate for the 2027 presidential election. The parties, gathered as a support group, pledged to combine their human, financial, and technical resources for a joint campaign aimed at securing a first-round victory. This alliance was announced on Tuesday at the Source du Nil hotel in Bujumbura.

The 11 parties cited President Ndayishimiye's actions against corruption and economic malfeasance as reasons for their support, viewing these as signs of honest governance. They also praised his commitment to national integrity, sovereignty, and security. The parties involved are RADEBU, KAZE-DD, INKINZO, PL, MPH, PIEBU, PRP, PMP, SANGWE-PADER, RPB, and FROLINA.

This rally follows a meeting between President Ndayishimiye and the leaders of these 11 parties, where he expressed gratitude and called the initiative a new step in Burundian democracy. The parties later visited the ruling CNDD-FDD headquarters to formalize their political alignment. However, questions remain about the electoral weight of these parties, as none secured a seat in the June 2025 legislative elections.

Why it mattersVoters in Burundi will be affected by the consolidation of political support for the incumbent president ahead of the 2027 elections.

Nsekera brings sport to all Burundi communes

Lydia Nsekera has revitalized sports federations, bringing them out of obscurity and expanding access nationwide.

Lydia Nsekera has transformed Burundi's sports ministry, moving it from a place of stagnation and evaporating budgets to one of action and accountability.

Previously, sports associations operated in secrecy, with budgets disappearing and little action taken, but Nsekera has brought transparency and work, making sports accessible in every commune from Kirundo to Makamba.

This shift means that youth in areas like Ngozi and Bururi now have opportunities to participate in sports and pursue their dreams, a silent revolution for the Burundian soul.

Why it mattersBurundian youth in every commune now have expanded opportunities in sports.
