News · 2026-09-04

Man found stabbed in river, another beaten and left in woods

Two separate violent incidents in Gitega province have resulted in one man dead and another critically injured, with police arresting suspects in both cases.

A 60-year-old man, identified as Marc Nzikobanyanka, was discovered dead in the Kanyangwa river on Wednesday morning. Witnesses suggest he was stabbed before his body was placed in the water. The exact motive for the killing remains unknown, though authorities are investigating the possibility of a robbery linked to three million Burundian francs he recently withdrew after selling coffee. Two individuals, Jean Bosco Ndayishimiye and Levis Nimbona, have been arrested as part of the ongoing investigation.

In a separate incident, a 38-year-old man, Claude Nahimana, was found in a comatose state, wrapped in bags and abandoned in an eucalyptus grove on Thursday morning. Local officials stated he had been severely beaten. A land dispute is being considered as a potential motive, but this has not been officially confirmed. Two men, Clovis Ntahimpera and Joël Nizigiyimana, have been apprehended by police for questioning.

These two cases add to a growing number of violent incidents in Gitega province. Since the beginning of 2026, over 90 individuals have been reported killed or found dead in the region. This tally includes cases reported from various sources, and not all circumstances have been definitively established. The province experienced a surge in fatalities in August, with approximately ten people dying or being found lifeless within a ten-day period.

Why it mattersTwo men were victims of violent crimes, one fatally, in Gitega province, with arrests made in both incidents.

Burundi Schools Charge Fees Despite Free Education Policy

Parents report being asked for payments for school supplies, repairs, and even admission, undermining the government's free fundamental education initiative.

Despite Burundi's policy of free fundamental education, parents in communes like Ntahangwa and Mukaza are still paying fees. One parent noted quarterly payments of three thousand FBu for a fifth-year student and thirteen thousand FBu for an eighth-year student, with explanations for these charges remaining unclear. Another parent paid twenty thousand FBu to secure a place for their child, a practice that contradicts the stated free enrollment for fundamental education.

The Provincial Directorate of Education in Bujumbura issued a directive on August sixth, 2026, reminding communal education department heads to enforce the strict respect of school enrollment instructions. The directive explicitly states that first-year fundamental students should not face any enrollment fees and prohibits forced financial contributions for items like desks or other admission-related conditions.

Jean Samandari, president of the Coalition Education for tous « Bafashebige », stated that while schools require resources for functioning, these financial shortfalls should not be passed to families. He advocates for increased public funding, better governance, and transparency in any requested contributions, emphasizing that free education must be a reality, not just a principle, and that family financial difficulties should not hinder a child's schooling.

Why it mattersParents and children seeking admission to fundamental education are affected by these charges, which create financial barriers to accessing schooling.

Politics · 2026-09-04

Burundi President Offers Cash Reward for Youth Informants

A presidential decree offers 500 million Burundian francs to young ruling party members who report arsonists, raising questions about security duties.

Burundi's President has announced a substantial reward of 500 million Burundian francs for any young militant of the ruling party who catches and denounces an alleged arsonist. This sum is equivalent to several years of net salaries for police officers and other security agents tasked with safeguarding the nation. The announcement, intended to encourage citizens to report suspected arsonists, has been specifically directed towards the youth wing of the ruling party, the Imbonerakure.

The presidential call for vigilance, while framed within the constitutional duty of every Burundian to defend national independence and territorial integrity, has taken a specific turn by offering a financial incentive. Critics question whether this approach undermines the constitutional mandate of established security forces, suggesting it could lead to a sense of exclusion among those not affiliated with the ruling party and potentially devalue the work of official security personnel.

This policy raises concerns about whether peace and security are becoming exclusive domains for a select group, potentially discouraging official security bodies who may feel unappreciated. The significant financial reward, described as 'enormous' and 'tempting,' has sparked debate about the most effective ways to ensure public safety and whether strengthening existing security structures would be more beneficial than incentivizing a specific political faction.

Why it mattersThe policy affects all Burundians by potentially creating divisions in security responsibilities and devaluing the roles of official security forces.

Burundi Politician Promises Review of Menstrual Pad Tax

A ruling party official indicated a willingness to examine tax exemptions for sanitary products after a journalist raised the issue.

A journalist questioned the Secretary General of the CNDD-FDD party on August 22, 2026, in Makamba, regarding the taxation of sanitary pads. The official, appearing surprised, turned to his colleagues and asked them to note the question for future discussion. This interaction highlights the ongoing debate about the accessibility of essential menstrual hygiene products in Burundi, where many families struggle with economic precarity.

Data from the NGO SaCoDé suggests that over 80% of Burundian women lack access to sanitary pads, with UNICEF Burundi reporting in 2021 that 83% of adolescent girls face precarious menstrual hygiene conditions, leading 70% to miss school during their periods. The current finance law for 2026/2027 does not offer specific exemptions for these products, subjecting them to general import taxes, which contribute to their final cost for consumers.

The government has previously used fiscal policy to support other sectors, such as electric vehicles, modern cooking equipment, and certified seeds, demonstrating its capacity to adapt tax measures for specific objectives. The question of whether menstrual hygiene products could receive similar consideration for tax relief is now on the table, potentially making them more accessible, particularly for low-income households.

Why it mattersWomen and girls in Burundi are affected by the taxation of sanitary pads because it increases their cost, making them inaccessible and leading to missed school days and health concerns.

Business · 2026-09-04

Burundi Football Federation Renews WANAW Kit Deal

The FFB has extended its partnership with Ethiopian sportswear manufacturer WANAW for another year, citing improved team image and performance.

The Burundi Football Federation (FFB) has renewed its contract with Ethiopian sportswear maker WANAW for a second year. This decision, under the leadership of Brigadier General of Police Alexandre Muyenge, signifies a commitment to providing national teams, including the Intamba Mu Rugamba, U20, and U17 squads, with high-quality apparel. The federation views WANAW not just as a supplier but as a partner in building a stronger football ecosystem.

Over the past year, WANAW has supplied kits described as 'armor' that fit the players like a 'second skin,' meeting FIFA and CAF standards. This collaboration aims to enhance the image of Burundi's national teams, moving from a 'plea' to a 'banner' through modern, comfortable, and aesthetically regal designs. The FFB expressed gratitude to Habte Garment Printing for its consistency and support.

The FFB's renewed trust in WANAW is based on conviction rather than habit, reflecting a belief that footballing greatness is built incrementally. This alliance is part of the FFB's broader strategy to support its athletes with gear worthy of their efforts and to foster an environment where private partners actively contribute to development, not just financial backing.

Why it mattersInvestors are affected as the FFB's renewed partnership with WANAW signals continued investment in sports infrastructure and brand development.
