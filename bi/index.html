Ndayishimiye files for second term as opposition leaders stay out of the race

Burundi's president became the first candidate to file for the May 2027 presidential vote, as 11 small parties backed him.

Burundi's president, Évariste Ndayishimiye of the ruling CNDD-FDD party, filed his candidacy on Friday at the Independent National Electoral Commission (CENI) in Bujumbura, the economic capital. He is seeking a second seven-year term in the election set for 3 May 2027. He was the first to submit his papers, followed by Abel Gashatsi, candidate of the UPRONA party. The CENI will accept applications until 22 November.

Candidates must provide identity documents, photographs, a medical certificate, a list of 200 supporters and a caution of 100 million Burundian francs. The supporter list must reflect the country's gender, ethnic and regional diversity, though supporters need not belong to the candidate's party. Ndayishimiye warned local administrative officials that they would be held responsible for any disturbances in their areas.

Four other figures have said they intend to run: Gabriel Banzawitonde of the APDR, Francis Rohero of the FPI, Gaspard Kobako of AND Intadohoka and Valentin Kavakure of the FPN Imboneza. Their filings have not yet been confirmed. On 11 November, 11 parties announced their support for the president, pledging to pool their staff, funds and technical resources. None of them won a seat in the June 2025 legislative elections.

Why it mattersOpposition candidates who want to stand must gather 200 supporters and pay a 100 million franc deposit before the 22 November deadline.

Burundi signs El Niño plan as 630.2 billion francs needed for 4.7 million people

The government has so far committed 10 billion francs, far short of the estimated total cost of the response.

Burundi's government and the United Nations resident coordinator in Burundi, Violette Kakyomya, signed a joint declaration on Friday, 13 November 2026, on an operational plan for the El Niño phenomenon covering 2026 to 2027. The plan estimates the total cost of needs at 630.2 billion Burundian francs (BIF).

About 4.7 million people are exposed to flood risks and other known shocks, according to a risk analysis carried out jointly by the National Platform for Risk Prevention and Disaster Management and the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA). Foreign Affairs Minister Edouard Bizimana said the first disasters, including strong winds, torrential rains, hail and floods, have already been recorded in some regions. Seasonal forecasts for September to December 2026 point to above-normal rainfall across all natural regions.

The government has already set aside a package of 10 billion BIF, although it had earlier announced 20 billion BIF on 29 October. Bizimana said the 10 billion francs was only a start and that the government is still raising funds from development partners, diplomats, the private sector and international organisations.

Why it mattersFarmers and families in flood-prone areas face the risk of losing crops and homes, and the response is still short of funds, with the government saying the total cost is 630.2 billion BIF.

Rumonge commune threatens 130 families with land seizure

Communal authorities want to reclaim about 600 hectares on Mutambara hill, land residents have farmed for decades.

About 130 families, including roughly 10 widows, living on Mutambara hill in the Gatete zone of Rumonge commune, in Burunga province in south-western Burundi, say communal authorities are threatening to expropriate their land. They have been locked in a land dispute with the administration since 2023 over parcels they have worked for several decades.

The dispute centres on where the boundary lies between state land and the plots the residents occupy and cultivate, an area estimated at about 600 hectares. Residents say their plots were allocated under rural development programmes, including the PIA-RU.BU.BU, and that they received cession contracts although the state kept ownership. They have farmed the land without interruption since the programme was dissolved, growing food crops and oil palms, and some plots have passed through inheritance or been sold between individuals.

The families are asking the authorities to clarify the limits between public land and the land they claim, to end a conflict they say threatens their livelihoods.

Why it mattersIf the authorities proceed, the families stand to lose the food crops and oil palms on which they depend, and the parcels they have inherited or bought.

Security · 2026-11-14

Congolese refugee found hanged near Burundi camp

Cadeau Serugo's body showed signs of torture, including bound hands and a bruised face, with his money and phone missing.

A Congolese refugee, Cadeau Serugo, was found dead, hanged from a tree, on Friday in the Minyare area, near the Kavumu camp in eastern Burundi's Cankuzo province. His body bore marks of violence, and an investigation has been opened into the circumstances of his death. Serugo, originally from South Kivu in the Democratic Republic of Congo, was registered at the Musasa camp but had been living at Kavumu camp with his family for several months.

Local testimonies indicate Serugo had traveled to Musasa to collect his November food assistance, a process requiring physical presence at his registered camp. On his return journey by motorcycle taxi, he was reportedly ambushed in the isolated Minyare zone. Sources described his hands as tied, his face bruised, and his clothes torn, suggesting torture. His food assistance money and phone were also reported missing.

The body was taken to the Cankuzo morgue for an autopsy, and while an investigation is underway, no arrests had been made by Saturday. Serugo's family is demanding that the circumstances of his death be clarified and that security be improved on the Cankuzo-Minyare-Kavumu route. The incident highlights the difficulties faced by refugees living away from their official registration camps.

Why it mattersThe family of Cadeau Serugo, a Congolese refugee, is demanding justice and increased security on the route to Kavumu camp after his death.

Business · 2026-11-14

Gitega's loud ads drive away customers

Businesses in Burundi's capital report lost sales due to incessant advertising noise.

Loud, repetitive advertising from a digital billboard in Gitega, Burundi, is disrupting daily life and harming businesses. The sound, described as strident and deafening, plays on a loop for months, forcing pedestrians to cover their ears and making normal conversation impossible.

Taxi drivers report losing customers because the noise prevents them from negotiating fares, and nearby hotels are experiencing complaints from guests seeking quiet. Shopkeepers and boutique owners also note that customers cut their visits short, finding the advertising repellent rather than attractive.

Residents and local doctors are concerned about the long-term health effects of constant high-decibel noise, including chronic stress, sleep disturbances, and hearing damage. The population is appealing to the administration for intervention to stop the disruptive advertising.

Why it mattersBusinesses in Gitega are losing customers and income due to disruptive advertising noise, with residents also facing potential health risks.
