Burundi man found hanged, neighbours suspect murder

Emmanuel Barahinduka, 52, was discovered on Sunday, October 25, in Gitega province, with residents demanding an independent investigation.

The body of 52-year-old Emmanuel Barahinduka was found hanging in his home on Mugaruro hill, Gishubi commune, Gitega province, on Sunday, October 25. While the local administrator suggested suicide, many residents dispute this, suspecting foul play and calling for an independent inquiry into the circumstances of his death.

Witnesses reported Barahinduka was found suspended by a rope from his house's rafters, but the motives remain unclear. The Mugaruro hill chief, Emmanuel Ntandikiye, stated it was a suicide, a claim rejected by residents who believe he may have been killed before being hanged. These residents mentioned potential land disputes as a possible motive, though they provided no public evidence linking them to the death.

This incident follows the death of 43-year-old Félix Nsabimana on October 15 in the same commune, who died after a grenade explosion at his home. SOS Médias Burundi has recorded approximately 100 deaths in Gitega province since the start of 2026, raising security concerns and calls for thorough investigations.

Why it mattersResidents of Gitega province are concerned about unexplained deaths and demand independent investigations into suspicious circumstances.

Burundi farmers protest fertiliser shortages

Farmers in Butanyerera province are planting seeds without vital fertilisers due to distribution delays and insufficient quantities.

Farmers in Burundi are facing uncertainty as delays in fertiliser distribution by FOMI, coupled with insufficient quantities and issues with refunds for undelivered products, threaten crop yields. With the A cultivation season underway and rains encouraging planting, many farmers are forced to sow seeds without any fertiliser. This situation is making food prices increasingly inaccessible for households and raises fears of reduced harvests and worsening food insecurity.

In Matongo commune, Butanyerera province, farmers protested on October 26 against the distribution methods of FOMI fertilisers, specifically the division of a 25-kilo bag between two beneficiaries, which they deemed inadequate. The protest on the Bukeye-Kayanza road ended after a FOMI truck arrived, facilitating discussions to resolve the dispute. The governor of Butanyerera, Gaspard Segasago, acknowledged the protest and urged calm.

In Musasa, Kiremba commune, also in Butanyerera province, farmers are beginning their planting without the fertilisers they ordered. Some are sowing seeds without any fertiliser, unable to wait longer, despite knowing that depleted soils require mineral fertilisers and organic matter for good production. Congolese refugees in the Musasa camp are also affected, as they lack livestock for manure and must purchase compost in small amounts.

Why it mattersFarmers in Burundi are unable to access essential fertilisers, risking crop failure and food insecurity.

Burundi minister warns against fake recruiters

Foreign Affairs Minister Édouard Bizimana urges job seekers to verify contracts before leaving Burundi.

Poverty and unemployment in Burundi are pushing citizens to seek work abroad, but fake recruiters are trapping them with false promises. These deceptive agents can lead to joblessness, exploitation, or even recruitment into armed groups. Minister of Foreign Affairs Édouard Bizimana advised job seekers on October 26 to verify their contracts and working conditions before departing.

Bizimana stated that many Burundians discover upon arrival that conditions do not match what was promised, leaving some without employment or at risk of being drawn into armed groups. He recommended that candidates with doubts seek information from competent services before traveling and urged parents to check their children's departure conditions.

The minister also encouraged Burundian workers abroad to register with their country's embassy or diplomatic representation for assistance. This warning comes amid concerns over the practices of migrant worker recruitment agencies in Burundi, with Senate President Gervais Ndirakobuca having previously denounced their proliferation.

Why it mattersBurundian citizens seeking work abroad are being trapped by fake recruiters, facing exploitation or recruitment into armed groups.

Sport · 2026-10-28

Yussuf Ndayishimiye rises from local leagues to OGC Nice

Burundian footballer Yussuf Ndayishimiye joined OGC Nice in France after a career path through Turkish clubs.

Yussuf Ndayishimiye, born October 27, 1998, began his football journey at Saint-Augustin, initially not selected for a tournament at age nine.

He later played for Mukaza in the third division before joining Les Lierres in the second division, where he earned a place in the starting 11 after 20 minutes in his sixth match.

Ndayishimiye joined Aigle Noir in the 2016-2017 season after a call from Révérien Ndikuriyo, then moved to Yeni Malatyaspor in Turkey from 2019 to 2021, before signing with OGC Nice in France.

Why it mattersBurundian footballer Yussuf Ndayishimiye's career progression affects national pride and international representation.
