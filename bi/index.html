Frodebu demands justice for Ndadaye assassination

The Sahwanya Frodebu party will not field a candidate in the 2027 presidential election due to lack of transparency.

33 years after the assassination of Melchior Ndadaye, Burundi's first democratically elected Hutu president, the Sahwanya Frodebu party is still demanding that all those responsible for his 1993 murder be identified and convicted. The party announced it will not present a candidate in the May 2027 presidential election, citing insufficient guarantees of transparency and inclusivity in the electoral process. Phénias Niyigaba, the party's vice-president, stated that justice for the assassination remains incomplete.

While acknowledging some progress, including the reopening of the case, arrests, and convictions, Frodebu insists that all alleged perpetrators and masterminds must be brought to justice. The party has filed a complaint but found no satisfactory response. Ndadaye, founder of Sahwanya Frodebu, was assassinated less than four months into his presidency during a coup attempt. His death plunged Burundi into a prolonged period of violence and civil war.

In 2020, the Supreme Court of Burundi sentenced 17 of the 21 individuals prosecuted in connection with Ndadaye's assassination to life imprisonment. The Sahwanya Frodebu party continues to call for full accountability for all those involved in the planning and execution of the assassination, believing justice is still incomplete. The current presidential couple, Évariste and Angeline Ndayishimiye, paid respects at Ndadaye's tomb.

Why it mattersThe Sahwanya Frodebu party is demanding justice for the 1993 assassination of its founder, Melchior Ndadaye, and boycotting the 2027 presidential election.

Burundi official dies in unclear circumstances at ADB

Bruce Sebanza was buried without an autopsy amid allegations of torture and calls for investigation.

Bruce Sebanza, an official at the Burundi Development Agency (ADB), died on October 13, 2026, at the agency's premises in Bujumbura. He was buried on Wednesday, October 21, without a public autopsy, prompting questions about the circumstances of his death. The National Independent Human Rights Commission (CNIDH) visited the ADB to investigate and requested official inquiries into the matter.

During a service in Bujumbura, Sebanza's mother described his death as "barbaric torture" and praised his professionalism. Relatives who went to the Kamenge military hospital on October 13 reported finding his body transported in a government pickup truck, unclothed except for a soiled singlet and bloody underwear. They noted his body was still warm and showed signs of abrasions consistent with being dragged.

Witnesses reported hearing distress calls from the ADB premises on the night of October 12 to October 13. Some accounts from the morgue staff suggested physical marks on the body could indicate constriction, possibly by a rope. No independent medical or legal conclusions on the cause of death have been made public.

Why it mattersThe death of Bruce Sebanza, an ADB official, is being investigated due to allegations of torture and suspicious circumstances surrounding his burial.

Burundi football league kicks off ninth round

Eight teams will compete in five matches on Sunday, with three games also scheduled for Friday and Saturday.

The ninth round of Burundi's Primus Ligue kicks off on Friday with Muzinga FC hosting Ngozi City at Urunani stadium at three o'clock in the afternoon. Also on Friday, Vision FC will play Inter Star at Intwari stadium at the same time. Rukinzo FC faces Aigle Noir on Saturday at Urunani stadium.

Sunday features five simultaneous matches starting at three o'clock in the afternoon across the country. Bumamuru will play Kayanza United at Urunani, Messager Ngozi faces Young Star at Urukundo, and Panthère Noire plays Flambeau du Centre at NPPC.

The final two matches on Sunday are Musongati FC versus Olympic Star at Ingoma stadium, and Never Give Up against Vital'o FC at Intwari stadium. These matches are part of the 2026-2027 season.

Why it mattersFootball fans in Burundi will watch their favorite teams compete in the ninth round of the Primus Ligue.

Burundi police general detained with three officers

General Romuald Ndababishije, deputy migration commissioner, was arrested on October 17 in Bujumbura amid unclear circumstances.

Burundi police general Romuald Ndababishije, deputy commissioner general of migrations, was detained on October 17 in Bujumbura. Three other police officers, including his driver, were arrested before the general. The first officer was reportedly arrested on October 14, and the driver in Kayanza province.

Police sources suggest the detentions may be linked to passports and involve a presidential office official, though this remains unverified. A tense atmosphere prevails at the migration headquarters in northern Bujumbura. The police and administrative authorities have not officially commented on the reasons for the arrests or the charges.

The detentions occur as Burundi approaches an election period, with sources indicating rivalry for Ndababishije's strategic position. The conditions of detention and whether family visits have occurred are unknown. No official statement has been released regarding the arrests or potential charges.

Why it mattersGeneral Romuald Ndababishije and three other police officers are detained in Burundi.

Saido Berahino retires from Premier League to coach Burundi

The former West Bromwich Albion forward announced his return to his birth country to train the national team.

Saido Berahino, who began his football journey in the streets of Bujumbura, has announced his retirement from professional play after a career that included time in the Premier League with West Bromwich Albion.

Berahino stated that football gave him everything, from the dusty streets of his youth to the green pitches of England, and he expressed deep gratitude to West Bromwich Albion for his development.

He has now decided to hang up his boots, accepting that the time is right, and will return to Burundi to serve the national team, the Intamba Mu Rugamba, as a coach on the field.

Why it mattersSaido Berahino, a native of Bujumbura, has retired from professional football and will coach Burundi's national team.
