Paris loses to Barcelona in four-goal thriller

Two early injuries to key players did not deter Paris from a spirited comeback, ultimately falling short in a high-scoring match.

The match began with an immediate threat from Khvicha Kvaratskhelia, whose shot struck the post within the first two minutes. However, the game took a turn when Paris lost two players to injury: Ousmane Dembélé in the 19th minute and Dani Olmo in the 27th minute. Despite these setbacks, young talent Désiré Doué opened the scoring for Paris in the 38th minute with a precise strike.

In the second half, Barcelona, previously undefeated under coach Hansi Flick, equalized in the 55th minute through Xavi Espart's powerful shot. Paris faced further challenges as Marquinhos and Kvaratskhelia were forced off, leaving their team vulnerable. Yet, Lucas Hernandez headed in a goal from a corner in the 72nd minute to put Paris back in the lead.

The lead was short-lived as Barcelona's Lamine Yamal scored in the 79th minute, bringing the score to 2-2. The match concluded with Barcelona securing a 4-2 victory, despite Paris's resilience and earlier efforts to overcome injuries and the deficit.

Burundi death certificate questions agency premises

An administrative document states a Burundi Development Agency official died at home, but his actual place of death was the agency's office.

An administrative certificate stating that Bruce Sebanza, an official at the Burundi Development Agency (ADB), died at home has raised questions. The document, confirmed as authentic by sources, designates the ADB's director general, Dr. Révérien Nizigiyimana, as the death declarant. However, the certificate states Sebanza died "at home (within the premises of the A.D.B.)" on October 13, 2026, despite his actual residence being in a different part of Bujumbura.

The discrepancy between Sebanza's registered address in Mutakura and the ADB's location in Mutanga Nord, both in the northern part of the capital, casts doubt on the circumstances under which the certificate was issued. Nizigiyimana's role as declarant is also notable, as his name has been previously linked to testimonies concerning events preceding Sebanza's death, prompting calls for a rigorous examination of his involvement.

This administrative attestation follows a call by the Independent National Human Rights Commission for investigations into the matter. The certificate does not replace a judicial inquiry to establish the exact causes of death and any potential responsibilities. Judicial authorities are expected to conduct thorough, independent, and impartial investigations.

Why it mattersThe death certificate's questionable details affect the family of Bruce Sebanza by obscuring the true circumstances of his death.

Saido Berahino retires from English football to coach Burundi

The former West Bromwich Albion striker returns to his birth nation to train the national team, the Intamba Mu Rugamba.

Saido Berahino has announced his retirement from professional football in England, concluding a career that began in the streets of Bujumbura. He expressed deep gratitude to West Bromwich Albion, stating that football has given him everything, from his early days as a refugee to playing in the Premier League. Berahino reflected on the highs and lows of his career, acknowledging the harsh lessons learned at the highest level of the sport.

The decision to hang up his boots comes not from weariness but from a sense of accomplishment, as Berahino prepares for a new chapter. He declared that football has remained his constant pillar throughout his life. His return to Burundi signifies a desire to give back to the nation where his journey began.

Berahino will now serve the Burundian national team, the Intamba Mu Rugamba, not as a distant figurehead but as an active coach on the field. This move marks a full circle for the player, transitioning from a refugee child to a coach for his home country's team.

Why it mattersBurundian football fans and aspiring players will be affected as Saido Berahino returns to coach the national team.

Burundi refugees ordered back to camps

Authorities cite security concerns for the order, but refugees fear loss of income and increased hardship.

Burundi's Office for Refugees and Stateless Persons (ONPRA) is compelling refugees living outside designated camps to return to their original settlements. ONPRA coordinator Richard Uwimana stated the measure is intended to enhance security and control over the refugee population amidst regional instability. This directive follows the recent public presentation of 13 individuals, primarily Banyamulenge refugees, accused of espionage for Rwanda and armed groups in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo.

The decision has sparked anger and worry among refugees who rely on activities outside the camps for their livelihoods. With reduced humanitarian aid and insufficient food rations, many families depend on small businesses, day labor, or other income-generating ventures in urban areas like Bujumbura to supplement their essential needs. A refugee selling clothes in Bujumbura expressed concern that the order will eliminate his primary source of income.

The government's push for refugees to return to camps is framed as a security imperative, particularly after accusations of spying were leveled against Banyamulenge refugees. However, the refugees themselves argue that these measures threaten their survival by cutting off vital income streams. Some relatives of those accused have dismissed the charges as unjust, stating the individuals fled conflict in South Kivu to protect their lives.

Why it mattersRefugees are affected by being forced to return to camps, potentially losing their sources of income and facing greater hardship.
