Cholera spreads in Burundi's capital

Water shortages and difficult hospital access worsen fears of a growing epidemic in Bujumbura.

Cholera cases are increasing in Bujumbura, Burundi's economic capital, with patients being admitted to Roi Khaled and Prince Louis Rwagasore hospitals. The disease has been confirmed in the Gihosha area, raising concerns about its spread. A patient at Roi Khaled hospital reported a constant influx of cholera patients since the previous week, with new cases appearing daily.

Residents in parts of Gihosha and surrounding areas are also experiencing a significant lack of clean water, which exacerbates fears of cholera transmission. The hospital patient expressed worry about potential contamination from cholera sufferers during lengthy registration procedures before medical assessment. They urged faster examination of new arrivals to isolate cholera cases and protect other patients.

The cholera outbreak in Bujumbura occurs as the disease also affects other regions, including Kizuka and Gatete in Rumonge province, where about 10 cases were reported in late October. Seven people remained hospitalized, one died at home, and three recovered. Poor hygiene and lack of safe water are key factors in cholera's spread, a disease that has seen recurrent outbreaks in Burundi since late 2022.

Why it mattersCholera is spreading in Bujumbura, and water shortages are making the situation worse for residents and hospital patients.

Politics · 2026-11-08

Nathan Ntahondi elected head of Burundi media regulator

The new CNC president aims to modernize the institution and foster dialogue with journalists.

Nathan Ntahondi was unanimously elected president of Burundi's National Council for Communication (CNC) on November 6, 2026. He received all 15 votes from the council members, succeeding Espérance Ndayizeye who had held the position since 2024. Léonce Ntakirutimana was elected vice-president, replacing the late Laurent Kaganda.

Ntahondi, a communications and ICT specialist formerly with Publications de presse burundaise (PPB) and the ARCT telecom regulator, pledged to modernize the CNC's operations. He stated his commitment to making the CNC a modern institution that is close to the media, just in its decisions, and protective of journalists, regulating through dialogue before sanctions.

The election occurred as Reporters Without Borders (RSF) ranked Burundi 119th out of 180 countries in its 2026 World Press Freedom Index, a slight improvement from the previous year. Ntahondi aims to adapt the institution to digital developments and improve relations with media professionals through regular exchanges.

Why it mattersBurundian journalists and media professionals will be directly affected by the new leadership's approach to regulation and dialogue.

Agriculture · 2026-11-08

Burundi farmers protest fertilizer distribution

Farmers in Matongo blocked a national road over reduced fertilizer quantities and broken promises.

Burundi's Prime Minister Nestor Ntahontuye stated on October 30, 2026, that citizens are satisfied with the equal distribution of fertilizers. He acknowledged some concerns about incomplete deliveries but said explanations satisfied farmers.

However, farmers in Matongo, Butanyerera province, blocked the RN I national road with tree trunks. They protested that local authorities planned to distribute a 25kg bag of fertilizer to be shared by two farmers, amounting to 12.5kg each, instead of the promised 16kg per farmer.

This distribution method has caused frustration, particularly for farmers with large plots who find the 16kg allocation insufficient for their needs. The government's claim of satisfaction contrasts with local reports of discontent and protests.

Why it mattersFarmers are frustrated by insufficient and unevenly distributed fertilizer, impacting their ability to cultivate their land effectively.

Woman sentenced to life for husband's murder in Karusi

Joséphine Ntakarutimana must also pay 20 million Burundian francs to the victim's two children.

Joséphine Ntakarutimana was sentenced to life imprisonment on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, by the High Court of Karusi for the murder of her husband, Salvator Mburizi. The court also ordered her to pay 20 million Burundian francs in damages to the victim's two children born out of wedlock. Ntakarutimana fatally stabbed her husband on Rubuga hill in Karusi commune, Gitega province.

The murder occurred amidst marital tensions stemming from Mburizi's extramarital affairs, which resulted in two children outside his marriage to Ntakarutimana. The accused admitted to the charges and acknowledged long-standing conflict in her relationship with her husband. The prosecution had requested a life sentence, and the court followed this recommendation after deliberation.

The judicial decision was reportedly welcomed by many residents of Rubuga hill. Ntakarutimana, who has no children of her own, was tried in flagrante delicto.

Why it mattersJoséphine Ntakarutimana received a life sentence and was ordered to pay damages to her deceased husband's children.

Culture · 2026-11-08

Burundian diaspora celebrates culture in Canada

The tenth Ishaka cultural club performance took place at the National Arts Centre in Ottawa.

In October 2026, the Burundian diaspora in Ottawa and Gatineau, Canada, celebrated their culture through three key events. These included the tenth performance by the Ishaka cultural club at the National Arts Centre and an address by Burundian-Canadian Member of Parliament Arielle Kayabaga in the House of Commons. A conference on traditional values also took place at the University of Quebec in Outaouais.

MP Arielle Kayabaga highlighted Ishaka's tenth performance at the National Arts Centre during a session in the Canadian House of Commons, receiving applause from her colleagues. The events aimed to share Burundian cultural heritage with those living or born abroad. Preparations involved many Burundians in the Canadian capital, who were described as busy with the responsibility of universalizing their cultural legacy.

On October 1, an academic institution in Canada hosted a discussion on Burundian heritage and traditional values, attended by researchers, writers, students, and families. Participants discussed preserving traditional identity abroad amidst modernity. Young attendees shared their appreciation for values like respect and solidarity ('ugufashanya'), while organizers emphasized the role of parents in educating children about their heritage.

Why it mattersBurundian expatriates in Canada are actively engaged in cultural preservation and transmission to younger generations born abroad.
