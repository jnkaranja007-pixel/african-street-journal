Fire ravages Kinindo market in Bujumbura, destroying goods

A fire broke out in the Kinindo market in southern Bujumbura on Tuesday, destroying merchandise and causing significant losses for several traders, just days after a similar.

A fire erupted around 10 AM in the mattress section of Kinindo market, a key commercial hub in Burundi's economic capital. Firefighters, the Red Cross, and disaster management services responded quickly, bringing the blaze under control within an hour, but a portion of the goods was destroyed.

The incident follows a major fire last Friday that devastated two commercial galleries in downtown Bujumbura. Finance Minister Alain Ndikumana visited the scene, stating the fire's cause is unknown and an investigation has begun. He assured that the government would monitor the situation and consider support measures for affected traders.

Antoine Muzaneza, president of the Association of Traders of Burundi (ACOBU), urged authorities to enhance market security, proposing security committees, controlled opening hours, and surveillance cameras. Police increased security at Kinindo market to prevent looting amid the confusion caused by the fire.

Why it mattersTraders in Kinindo market, many of whom lost their livelihoods, are directly affected by the destruction of their goods and the ongoing uncertainty regarding market safety.

Police actions lead to two deaths in Bugendana, Gitega province

Two individuals died in Bugendana commune, Gitega province, following a police intervention and a violent assault, prompting local demands for independent investigations.

Désiré Nshimirimana, 25, died from a gunshot wound during a police intervention on Gaterama hill on Tuesday, August 25. Heavily armed police officers had gone to Joseph Bandora's home to make arrests related to a land dispute, according to a witness. The intervention escalated when residents opposed the police, leading to a police officer allegedly firing a shot that hit Nshimirimana in the chest, killing him instantly.

Following the incident, residents blocked the Bugendana-Kibimba road for nearly two hours, interrupting traffic. Six individuals—Duido Nahimana, Venant Ndayisenga, Olivier Nkurunziza, Remy Bigirimana, Édouard Bigirimana, and Rénovat Nimbona—were arrested and taken to police cells for investigation. Romuald Habonimana, chief of Gaterama hill, confirmed the events and the subsequent tension.

Separately, Isaac Habonimana, 39, died two days earlier on Sunday, August 23, on Gitongo hill after being severely beaten. Dieudonné Bizimana, chief of Gitongo hill, stated that Habonimana was accused of disturbing security in a bistro. Raphaël Nduwimana, the chief of the Gitongo police post, was identified as the alleged assailant and has since been arrested and taken to police cells in Bugendana.

Why it mattersResidents of Bugendana are directly affected by the deaths of two community members and the subsequent arrests, leading to demands for justice and independent investigations.

Burundi President Criticizes Mining, Commerce Ministries' Performance

President Ndayishimiye expressed deep disappointment with the commerce and mining sectors, stating they appear "non-existent" and contribute nothing to the country's wealth.

President Ndayishimiye, speaking on August fourteen, two thousand twenty-six, at Ntare House in Bujumbura, criticized high-ranking officials from the Ministry of Mineral Resources, Energy, Industry, Commerce, and Tourism. He stated that the commerce and mining sectors seem "non-existent" and contribute nothing to Burundi's wealth, despite their potential.

The President also targeted managers at Regideso, the public utility, for allegedly sharing dividends after budget exercises without contributing capital. He accused Regideso of corruption, citing instances where customers seeking electricity or meters were required to pay bribes. Ndayishimiye announced that the General Inspectorate would investigate Regideso's accounts.

Furthermore, the President lamented that the head of Sotrevo Mining Limited failed to execute an order worth one hundred forty-seven thousand dollars, despite sufficient liquidity. Sotrevo, which holds an industrial mining permit for Cassiterite, Colombo-Tantalite, and associated minerals in Murehe, was expected to be a significant revenue source for the country, but its management did not meet expectations.

Why it mattersBurundian citizens are affected by the perceived lack of contribution from key economic sectors and alleged corruption within public utilities, impacting national development and service delivery.

M23 and Twirwaneho Fighters Seize FARDC Positions in Bijombo

Clashes in Bijombo, South Kivu, led to the reported capture of several strategic locations previously held by Congolese forces and Wazalendo militias.

Fighters identified by local sources as affiliated with Twirwaneho and M23 reportedly took control of positions in Rubarati, Ruhuha, and Malimba on Sunday. These areas were previously occupied by elements of the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo (FARDC) and Wazalendo militiamen in the Bijombo groupement, Uvira territory. The information, which could not be independently verified, suggests a displacement of combatants from various camps following the confrontations.

Sources from the Masango area claim some fighters involved in the offensive arrived from Muramvya, Kashongo, Kageregere, and Mukumba. FARDC elements reportedly retreated towards the Munanira mountains, while Wazalendo militiamen moved towards Masango. Local sources also reported the abandonment of several weapons during the retreat. Representatives of civil society in Uvira territory further allege that Burundian Red Tabara fighters participated alongside Twirwaneho/M23 elements, though this claim remains unconfirmed.

Local analysts believe the potential capture of Malimba, Ruhuha, and Rubarati holds strategic importance for controlling the Uvira highlands and routes connecting several South Kivu localities. These positions could facilitate movements towards Bukavu, Kaziba, Rurambo, and Minembwe, and offer access to the Ruzizi plain and heights overlooking Uvira city. These assessments are analytical and do not confirm military progression into these areas.

Why it mattersResidents in the Uvira territory are directly affected by the ongoing conflict, facing displacement and potential changes in control over strategic areas.

Jadot Nkurunziza mobilizes youth for environmental cleanup in Bujumbura

Jadot Nkurunziza, known as "Giti," organizes young people in Ntahangwa commune to remove water hyacinth from Lake Tanganyika and promote urban cleanliness.

Jadot Nkurunziza, a native of Nyakabiga, is actively engaging residents in environmental efforts across Bujumbura. He is recognized for his work in the Ntahangwa commune, where he sensitizes youth on sanitation and flower planting along Mwezi Gisabo Boulevard, according to local residents.

His efforts extend to Lake Tanganyika, where he mobilizes young people to remove water hyacinth, which can threaten local activities and the ecosystem. Residents near the lake describe him as a "hero" for organizing these clean-up initiatives, which they say are uncommon in their community.

Nkurunziza's commitment to the environment stems from his childhood, where his grandfather taught him the importance of trees for fruits, respiration, energy, and construction materials. After studying environmental protection in Uganda, he returned to Burundi to apply his knowledge locally, including managing a nursery by the Ntahangwa River with various plants and trees.

Why it mattersResidents of Ntahangwa commune and those living near Lake Tanganyika are directly affected by improved cleanliness and the removal of invasive water hyacinth, which impacts their daily lives and local environment.
