Burundi governance group urges Ndayishimiye to change course before 2027 vote

The Parole et Actions pour le Réveil des Consciences et l'Évolution des Mentalités (PARCEM), a local governance group, says the next president must address inflation, shortages.

Source: SOS Medias Burundi · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Burundi's governance group Parole et Actions pour le Réveil des Consciences et l'Évolution des Mentalités (PARCEM), a local anti-corruption and governance organisation, urged President Évariste Ndayishimiye to acknowledge shortcomings in his record and revise his policies ahead of the 3 May 2027 presidential election. Its national director, Faustin Ndikumana, made the call at a news conference in Bujumbura on 24 November, saying the country needs a candidate with practical answers to its economic and social problems.

Ndikumana cited inflation, poverty, weak economic growth, particularly in agriculture, debt and low foreign-exchange reserves that limit imports. He also listed recurring shortages of fertiliser, fuel, water, electricity and medicines, alongside deteriorating roads and limited internet access. The group said the next president should respect the Constitution, ensure transparent public administration and declare assets as required by law.

10 candidates have filed with the national electoral commission, while 11 other parties support Ndayishimiye's bid for a second term. The group also called for leadership beyond party and religious divisions.

Why it mattersFarmers face weak agricultural growth and recurring fertiliser shortages among the problems the group says the next president must address.
