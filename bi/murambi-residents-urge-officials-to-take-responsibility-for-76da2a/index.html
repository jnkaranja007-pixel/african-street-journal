Murambi residents urge officials to take responsibility for fertiliser failures

Residents say farmers paid for fertiliser without receiving it, while some buyers order more than they need and resell the surplus.

Source: Iwacu · 2026-11-26 · 1 min read

Residents of Murambi, in Burundi’s Cibitoke province, say authorities have blamed individuals over fertiliser shortages instead of examining why farmers who paid were not served. They also point to farmers ordering more fertiliser than they need so they can resell the excess.

The residents say scapegoating can protect officials’ reputations or positions while leaving their own decisions unexamined. Pontien Bucumi, a local administrator, called on people to acknowledge failures and accept responsibility, saying that recognising weaknesses allows improvement.

Residents also cited the high cost of living and a father who blames his wife when relatives question his inability to pay for his children’s schooling or health care. They urged dialogue to find solutions and warned that blame can divide communities and hinder development.

Why it mattersFarmers who paid for fertiliser but were not served need authorities to examine the shortfall, while excess orders are being resold.
