Burundi’s communes urged to put youth jobs in annual budgets

Olucome’s secretary general says local authorities should work with communal councils to identify projects such as small industries and cooperatives.

Source: Iwacu · 2026-11-28 · 1 min read

A business leader in Burundi has urged communes to make youth employment a priority in their annual work and budget plans, as many young people struggle to find jobs. Arthémon Katihabwa, secretary general of the local business organisation Olucome, said communes should identify activities that can create work.

Katihabwa said communes should work with communal councils and other local administrators to find projects, including small industries and cooperatives. H., a 29-year-old graduate who is unemployed, said she had taken a shop job despite its low pay before losing it. N., 30, has moved through several small jobs and now works as a bus conductor in Bujumbura.

The source cited Burundi’s 2025 population census as showing that the country has a young majority. Katihabwa said some young people seek opportunities abroad, including in Arab countries and neighbouring states.

Why it mattersYoung people seeking work are the intended beneficiaries, while communes are being asked to identify employment projects through their annual plans.
